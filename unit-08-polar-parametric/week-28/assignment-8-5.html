Practice Set: Classifying the Snails
Assignment · 10 pts
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This set drills classifying limaçons by the ratio \(\frac{a}{b}\) — inner loop, cardioid, dimpled, or convex — and graphing lemniscates, including reasoning about where each curve is and is not defined. This is a practice set: scored for honest completion, with worked solutions released after the due date for line-by-line self-correction.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
A limaçon's whole personality — loop, dimple, or smooth curve — comes down to a single ratio. What does it mean that so much visual variety can be governed by one number?
📝 PROBLEM SET

Show your work in neat vertical form — label every value, and state the ratio \(\frac{a}{b}\) explicitly before you classify.

1
Classify and locate: Classify \(r=1+5\cos\theta\) using the ratio \(\frac{a}{b}\), then find every angle \(\theta\in[0,2\pi)\) where \(r=0\).
2
Classify and verify: Classify \(r=4+3\sin\theta\), then show algebraically that \(r\) never equals \(0\) for any \(\theta\).
3
Classify: Classify \(r=5+2\cos\theta\), stating the ratio \(\frac{a}{b}\) and comparing it to the convex boundary.
4
Graph a lemniscate: For \(r^2=9\cos2\theta\), find \(a\), state the domain (the values of \(\theta\in[0,2\pi)\) where the curve is defined), and build a table of \(r\) at \(\theta=0,\ \frac{\pi}{12},\ \frac{\pi}{6}\).
5
Find the domain: State the interval(s) of \(\theta\in[0,2\pi)\) for which \(r^2=25\sin2\theta\) is defined.
6
Explain why: In two or three sentences, explain why \(r^2=a^2\cos2\theta\) has no real points for values of \(\theta\) where \(\cos2\theta<0\), reasoning from what solving for \(r\) requires.
7
Honors stretch: For \(r=3+5\cos\theta\), find the two angles (to the nearest hundredth of a radian, or in exact form if possible) where the curve crosses the pole, then find the angular width of the inner loop — the difference between those two angles. Explain in one sentence what that angular width represents.
💡 CHECK YOURSELF WITH DESMOS
After classifying Problems 1–3 by hand, type each equation directly into r = ... form at desmos.com/calculator (Desmos plots any equation written in \(r\) and \(\theta\), typed as theta, as a polar curve automatically) and confirm by eye that the loop, dimple, or convex shape matches your classification. For Problem 4, graph r^2 = 9cos(2theta) and confirm the blank directions match the domain you found.
