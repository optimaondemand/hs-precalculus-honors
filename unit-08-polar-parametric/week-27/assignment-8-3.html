📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This is Week 27’s Focus assignment: using the symmetry tests to predict a polar graph’s shape before you build the table that proves it, then sketching the complete curve. The two gold-badged ⭐ METHOD PROBLEMS ask you to test all three symmetries explicitly, build a reduced table that the symmetry justifies, sketch the resulting curve, and either derive an unknown equation from given features or explain why a feature appears where it does. Your teacher reads them the way a careful reader checks a claimed derivation: every substitution shown, every symmetry conclusion justified, every claim verified.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
A symmetry test that passes lets you build half a table and trust the other half sight unseen. What in your work must make that trust obvious to someone else — not just a sketch that happens to look right?
📝 PROBLEM SET

Show your work for every problem in neat vertical form — one step per line, substitutions written out in full. On the method problems, show every symmetry substitution explicitly and never assert a conclusion without the algebra that produced it.

1
Warmup — table: Build a table for \(r=2\theta\) at \(\theta=0,\ \frac{\pi}{2},\ \pi,\ \frac{3\pi}{2},\ 2\pi\), and describe the resulting shape in one sentence.
2
Warmup — symmetry: Test \(r=5-5\cos\theta\) for symmetry about the polar axis, showing the substitution.
⭐ METHOD PROBLEM — your teacher grades this one for method
3
Table and symmetry together. Consider \(r=2-2\cos\theta\). ① Test the equation for symmetry about the polar axis, the line \(\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}\), and the pole, showing each substitution explicitly. ② Using whichever symmetry passed, build a table of \(r\) at \(\theta=0,\ \frac{\pi}{3},\ \frac{\pi}{2},\ \frac{2\pi}{3},\ \pi\) only, and explain why the symmetry justifies stopping there. ③ Sketch the complete curve, using the symmetry to complete the reflected half. ④ State the angle where the curve passes through the pole and the angle where it reaches its farthest point, and explain how your table shows both.
⭐ METHOD PROBLEM — your teacher grades this one for method
4
Working backward from features. A polar curve of the form \(r=a+a\sin\theta\) or \(r=a-a\sin\theta\) is known to be symmetric about the line \(\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}\), to pass through the pole exactly once, and to reach a maximum distance of \(6\) from the pole. ① Determine \(a\). ② Determine which sign matches a curve whose cusp points downward (toward \(\theta=\frac{3\pi}{2}\)), showing the substitution that proves it. ③ Write the complete equation and verify it by evaluating \(r\) at \(\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}\) and at \(\theta=\frac{3\pi}{2}\), confirming both the given maximum and the cusp location. ④ In one or two sentences, explain why a curve satisfying the \(\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}\) symmetry test could not have its cusp pointing left or right instead of up or down.
5
The AI cross-examination: Give your AI tutor the equation \(r=3-3\cos\theta\) with the instruction: “Test this equation for symmetry about the polar axis, the line \(\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}\), and the pole, and state which symmetries hold.” Copy the AI’s response onto your page, then verify each of its three claims yourself by performing the substitution. If the AI claims a symmetry that your own test fails, show the failing substitution and say so; if it misses a symmetry that your test confirms, show that too. Either outcome earns full credit; what is graded is the verification.
6
Honors stretch: Build a table for \(r=1+2\sin\theta\) at \(\theta=0,\ \frac{\pi}{6},\ \frac{\pi}{2},\ \frac{5\pi}{6},\ \pi,\ \frac{7\pi}{6},\ \frac{3\pi}{2},\ \frac{11\pi}{6},\ 2\pi\). Identify the interval of \(\theta\) where \(r\) is negative, and explain in two or three sentences what that negative stretch does to the plotted curve.
💡 CHECK YOURSELF WITH DESMOS
For Problems 3, 4, and 6, type each equation directly into desmos.com/calculator using theta for \(\theta\) (Desmos recognizes an \(r=\) expression as polar automatically) and trace the curve to confirm the cusp location, farthest point, and any inner loop your table predicted. The graph confirms; your table and symmetry work convince.

📊 GRADING RUBRIC

Your teacher applies this rubric to the two ⭐ method problems (the rest of the set is checked for honest completion). This same four-criterion standard governs every hand-graded item in this course, all year.

Criteria Excellent (4) Proficient (3) Developing (2) Beginning (1) Pts
Understanding of the material
4 pts		 All three symmetry tests are performed correctly; the table, sketch, and derived equation are all correct. Method is sound with a minor slip — e.g., one table value or a sign in the derived equation — that does not derail the solution. Partial grasp; a symmetry test is attempted but not completed, or the table is not actually justified by the symmetry claimed. Symmetry is assumed without a test, or the method does not match the mathematics being asked. 4 pts
Quality of reasoning
4 pts		 Every step follows from the last; the symmetry substitution is shown and its conclusion actually used to justify the reduced table, and the verification substitution is performed and interpreted. Reasoning is mostly explicit; one link in the chain (often the verification) is asserted rather than shown. Steps are present but read as recipe-following; explanations restate what without why. Answer appears with little or no supporting reasoning. 4 pts
Presentation & precision
4 pts		 Clean vertical layout, a clearly labeled table, a legible sketch; a stranger could verify the symmetry substitution without asking a clarifying question. Organized and legible with small lapses in labeling or alignment. Readable but disorderly; the grader must hunt for the symmetry substitution or a table value. Work is scattered or illegible; steps cannot be followed. 4 pts
Intellectual honesty
4 pts		 All work shown faithfully as done; uncertainty and gaps are named plainly; the AI verdict reflects genuine independent verification rather than assumed trust. Work is genuine; one rough patch is smoothed over rather than acknowledged. Some steps appear reverse-engineered from the answer rather than worked. The page misrepresents how the work was actually done. 4 pts
Total: 16 pts
