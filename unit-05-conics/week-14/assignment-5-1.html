📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This set opens Unit 5 the way Apollonius did — with the cone. You will submit your VR artifact from the Slicing the Cone session (the four labeled snapshots and your two-sentence claim), then work the family from both directions: tilt descriptions to conic names, and equations to classifications by inspection, with the degenerate cases and the function question from Unit 4 along the way. This is a practice set: scored for honest completion, with worked solutions released after the due date for line-by-line self-correction.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
One cone, one plane, four curves. What exactly about the tilt of the plane decides which curve appears — and how does the same verdict get printed in an equation's squared terms?
📝 PROBLEM SET

Show your work for every problem in neat vertical form — one step per line, final answers labeled. Where a problem asks for sentences, write full sentences; classification without a reason is half an answer.

🥽 VR ARTIFACT — include it in this PDF
From your Slicing the Cone Session in the Conic Sections Lab Location (join instructions are in Lesson 5-1): include your four labeled snapshots — circle, ellipse, parabola, hyperbola, each with its sticky note naming the conic and describing the plane's tilt relative to the cone's edge — and the snapshot of your two-sentence whiteboard claim connecting tilt to conic type. Place these as the first pages of your PDF. If you have not done the VR session yet, do it before starting problem 1 — problems 1, 6, and 7 lean on what your hands learned there.
1
The four slices: For each conic — circle, ellipse, parabola, hyperbola — write one sentence describing the cutting plane's tilt that produces it, phrased relative to the cone's axis and generators. (Your VR sticky notes are the rough draft; these four sentences are the fair copy.)
2
Classify by inspection (name the conic and cite the squared-term pattern that decides it):   ① \(x^2 + y^2 = 81\)   ② \(\dfrac{x^2}{36} + \dfrac{y^2}{16} = 1\)   ③ \(x^2 = -10y\)   ④ \(\dfrac{y^2}{25} - \dfrac{x^2}{9} = 1\).
3
Rearrange first, then classify (divide or regroup until the pattern shows, then name the conic):   ① \(4x^2 + 4y^2 - 64 = 0\)   ② \(9x^2 + y^2 - 9 = 0\)   ③ \(5y^2 - 20x = 0\)   ④ \(6x^2 - 4y^2 - 24 = 0\).
4
Degenerate cases: State exactly what each equation describes (show the algebra that proves it), and name which apex slice from the lesson it is:   ① \(x^2 + y^2 = 0\)   ② \(x^2 - y^2 = 0\)   ③ \(x^2 + y^2 = -9\).
5
Function or relation: Solve \(x^2 + y^2 = 81\) for \(y\), and explain in a sentence how the \(\pm\) in your result records the failure of the vertical line test. Then state which of the four whole conic types (drawn complete, in these standard positions) can ever pass that test, and why.
6
Hyperbola ≠ two parabolas: In two or three sentences, explain the geometric difference between a hyperbola's two branches and a pair of parabolas — use both the slicing picture (which tilts produce each) and the long-run behavior (asymptotes versus none).
7
Explain why: Two of the four conics occur at exactly one tilt each; the other two occur across whole ranges of tilt. Say which are which, and explain why — your VR session, where you nudged the plane a hair each way at the parabola, is the evidence.
8
Honors stretch — the flashlight: A flashlight throws a cone of light, so a wall is a cutting plane you can hold in your hand. Describe how to aim the flashlight at a wall to throw each of the four conics as the lit shape, justifying each aim by the tilt rule. Then explain the strangest case: when the flashlight is held parallel to the wall you see a hyperbola — but only one branch. Where is the other branch, and why does no light reach it? (Try it tonight with a real flashlight; report one sentence on what you saw.)
