📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This is Week 15's Focus assignment — the one set this week your teacher grades for method. The two gold-badged ⭐ METHOD PROBLEMS are the heart of the ellipse: the full two-radical derivation carried out on a specific curve, every squaring on the record, and a features-to-equation analysis in which \(b^2 = a^2 - c^2\) must be justified, not quoted. Your teacher reads them as arguments — each step following from the last, cross terms kept, the reversibility of each squaring addressed, verdicts written in sentences. Give every problem honest work, and give the method problems the whole derivation discipline with nothing skipped because it felt obvious.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Two pins, a loop of string, a pencil — and out comes \(\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1\). What exactly guarantees that the string and the equation draw the same curve?
📝 PROBLEM SET

Show your work for every problem in neat vertical form — one step per line, equals signs aligned, final answers labeled. Exact values throughout: \(3\sqrt{5}\), never 6.708. On every ellipse, sketch the right triangle that relates \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\) before you compute with it.

1
Equations from features: Write the standard-form equation of each ellipse:   ① foci \((\pm 3, 0)\), vertices \((\pm 5, 0)\)   ② vertices \((0, \pm 7)\), co-vertices \((\pm 2, 0)\) — and for ②, locate the foci exactly.
2
The definition, bare-handed: An ellipse has foci \((\pm 3, 0)\) and distance sum 10. Using distances only — no standard form — determine whether the point \((3, 3.2)\) lies on it. Show both distance computations in full and write a one-sentence verdict citing the definition.
⭐ METHOD PROBLEM — your teacher grades this one for method
3
The master derivation, on a real curve. An ellipse has foci \((\pm 4, 0)\) and distance sum 10. Starting from the bare definition — \(\sqrt{(x+4)^2 + y^2} + \sqrt{(x-4)^2 + y^2} = 10\) — derive its standard-form equation, with every stage labeled: ① isolate one radical; ② square, keeping the cross term, and simplify to the one-radical line; ③ square again and collect; ④ divide to standard form; ⑤ confirm honesty — one sentence per squaring explaining why both sides were nonnegative, so no ghost solutions entered; ⑥ verify that your equation's \(a\), \(b\), \(c\) satisfy \(b^2 = a^2 - c^2\), and check one vertex and one co-vertex against the original definition by direct distance arithmetic.
4
Vertical case: Write the equation of the ellipse with foci \((0, \pm 1)\) and distance sum 6, and state which denominator holds \(a^2\) and how you know.
⭐ METHOD PROBLEM — your teacher grades this one for method
5
Features, justified. An ellipse centered at the origin has vertices \((\pm 6, 0)\) and passes through the point \((3, 3)\). ① Find \(b^2\) by substituting the point, every algebra step shown; ② write the standard-form equation; ③ locate the foci exactly; ④ justify the triangle — do not quote \(b^2 = a^2 - c^2\); derive it for this ellipse by computing the two focal distances from the co-vertex \((0, b)\) and showing each equals \(a\), then naming the right triangle that results; ⑤ verdict sentence — state the full feature list (vertices, co-vertices, foci, distance sum) as a one-sentence portrait of the curve.
6
Explain why: The definition demands \(2a > 2c\) — the string longer than the distance between the pins. In three or four sentences: where does this condition enter the derivation's algebra (name the exact quantity whose sign it protects), what does the “ellipse” degenerate to if \(2a = 2c\), and why does \(2a < 2c\) produce no points at all?
7
Honors stretch: The focal chord of an ellipse is the vertical chord through a focus (the latus rectum, cousin of the parabola's). ① For \(\frac{x^2}{25} + \frac{y^2}{9} = 1\), find the exact endpoints and length of the chord through the focus \((4, 0)\). ② Then prove the general formula: for any \(\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1\), the focal chord has length \(\frac{2b^2}{a}\). (Substitute \(x = c\), use \(a^2 - c^2 = b^2\), and simplify — the fourth power that appears is not an accident.) ③ Confirm your part-① answer against the formula.
