📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This is Week 16's Focus assignment — the one set this week your teacher grades for method. Every second-degree equation on this page conceals a member of Apollonius's family, or its ghost, and your job is to name each one and prove the naming. The two gold-badged ⭐ METHOD PROBLEMS are the heart of the craft: one complete general-to-standard conversion of a hyperbola with every feature extracted and graphed, and one full trial of a suspect equation whose coefficients promise an ellipse the algebra must interrogate. Your teacher reads them as arguments — the coefficient prediction stated, every completed square balanced to the correct amount, every verdict written in a sentence. Give every problem honest work, and give the method problems the full procedure with nothing skipped because it felt obvious.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
The coefficients of \(x^2\) and \(y^2\) name a conic's species at a glance — yet sometimes the glance is wrong about everything but the name. What can completing the square see that the shortcut cannot?
📝 PROBLEM SET

Show your work for every problem in neat vertical form — one step per line, equals signs aligned, final answers labeled. On every completed square, show what was really added and pay it back explicitly. Draw graphs large, with scaffolding (boxes, asymptotes, axes) dashed and labeled. Exact values throughout.

1
The field guide: Classify each by inspecting \(A\) and \(C\) only — one line each, with the reason (“\(A = C\),” “opposite signs,” …):   ① \(7x^2 + 7y^2 - 14x + 28y - 35 = 0\)   ② \(5x^2 + 2y^2 + 10x - 8y - 27 = 0\)   ③ \(4y^2 - x^2 + 8y + 6x - 9 = 0\)   ④ \(x^2 + 6x - 4y + 1 = 0\).
2
Two conversions: Convert each to standard form and state the named features:   ① \(2x^2 + 2y^2 + 8x - 12y - 6 = 0\) (center and radius)   ② \(y^2 + 6y - 12x + 33 = 0\) (vertex, focus, directrix).
⭐ METHOD PROBLEM — your teacher grades this one for method
3
Take \(25x^2 - 4y^2 - 50x - 16y - 91 = 0\) through the complete discipline, every step labeled: ① predict — classify from \(A\) and \(C\), reason stated; ② convert — complete both squares, showing for each what was really added (coefficient times inserted constant) and paying back exactly that, signs and all; ③ extract — center, \(a\), \(b\), \(c\) (computation shown), vertices, foci, and asymptote equations; ④ graph — the five-step discipline from Lesson 5-10: dashed box, dashed asymptotes, darkened vertices, marked foci, branches that never touch their asymptotes; ⑤ verdict sentence — confirm or correct your step-① prediction, and state one feature of the graph the coefficients alone could never have told you.
4
Ellipse in camouflage: Convert \(9x^2 + 25y^2 - 36x + 150y + 36 = 0\) to standard form; state center, \(a\), \(b\), \(c\), and foci. One sentence: which relation gave you \(c\) here, and why is it not the one from problem 3?
5
Reverse gear: Convert \(\dfrac{(x+2)^2}{9} - \dfrac{y^2}{16} = 1\) to general form \(Ax^2 + Cy^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0\) with integer coefficients. Then check your own expansion: do your \(A\) and \(C\) predict the species you started from?
⭐ METHOD PROBLEM — your teacher grades this one for method
6
Put \(4x^2 + y^2 - 24x + 2y + 37 = 0\) on trial: ① the prediction — state what species \(A\) and \(C\) promise, with the reason; ② the interrogation — complete both squares in full, bookkeeping shown; ③ the verdict — state in a sentence what the graph actually is, and why the algebra forces that conclusion (what can a sum of two squares equal to your right-hand side actually permit?); ④ the family — now replace the constant 37 by a parameter \(F\), so the equation reads \(4x^2 + y^2 - 24x + 2y + F = 0\), and classify the graph for every real value of \(F\): the range of \(F\) giving a genuine ellipse, the single \(F\) giving a point, and the range giving no graph at all — each case justified from your completed-square form; ⑤ one sentence connecting the verdict to the lesson's motto: what did completing the square see here that the coefficient shortcut could not?
7
Explain why: In three or four sentences: why can the linear coefficients \(D\) and \(E\) never change a conic's species, while the constant \(F\) can erase the graph entirely? Argue from what completing the square actually does with each of them — where \(D\) and \(E\) end up, versus where \(F\) ends up.
8
Honors stretch (the collapsed hyperbola): Consider \(x^2 - y^2 + 4x + 4y = 0\). ① What do the coefficients predict? ② Complete both squares and show the equation becomes a difference of squares equal to zero; factor it completely. ③ State exactly what the graph is — give the equations of both pieces and their intersection point. ④ In two sentences, justify the description “a hyperbola collapsed onto its own asymptotes,” and name the slice of the double cone (from Lesson 5-1) that produces this figure. ⑤ Bonus thread: what would the analogous degenerate look like for a parabola, and what slice produces it?
💡 CHECK YOURSELF WITH DESMOS
After both method problems are done on paper, verify them in Desmos (desmos.com/calculator): type problem 3's general form exactly as given and confirm the curve, its vertices, and its asymptotes match your graph. Then type problem 6's equation and watch the screen show nothing — graph 4x^2 + y^2 - 24x + 2y + F = 0 with a slider on \(F\) and watch the ellipse shrink to your point and vanish precisely at the boundaries you computed. Here the pixels reach their limit and only the algebra testifies. The calculator confirms what has size; your algebra convinces about everything else.

📊 GRADING RUBRIC

Your teacher applies this rubric to the two ⭐ method problems (the rest of the set is checked for honest completion). This same four-criterion standard governs every hand-graded item in this course, all year.

Criteria Excellent (4) Proficient (3) Developing (2) Beginning (1) Pts
Understanding of the material
4 pts		 Method fits the problem exactly; definitions, notation, and results are used correctly throughout. Method is sound with a minor conceptual slip that does not derail the solution. Partial grasp; the approach starts correctly but loses the thread. The method does not match the mathematics being asked. 4 pts
Quality of reasoning
4 pts		 Every step follows from the last; the why is stated, and verification is actually performed. Reasoning is mostly explicit; one link in the chain is asserted rather than shown. Steps are present but read as recipe-following; explanations restate what without why. Answer appears with little or no supporting reasoning. 4 pts
Presentation & precision
4 pts		 Clean vertical layout, aligned equals signs, labeled answers, exact values; graphs read as visual arguments. Organized and legible with small lapses in labeling or alignment. Readable but disorderly; the grader must hunt for the thread. Work is scattered or illegible; steps cannot be followed. 4 pts
Intellectual honesty
4 pts		 All work shown faithfully as done; uncertainty and gaps are named plainly; nothing is dressed up. Work is genuine; one rough patch is smoothed over rather than acknowledged. Some steps appear reverse-engineered from the answer rather than worked. The page misrepresents how the work was actually done. 4 pts
