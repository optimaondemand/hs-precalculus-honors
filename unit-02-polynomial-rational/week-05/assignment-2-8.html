📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This is Week 5's Focus assignment — the one set this week your teacher grades for method. The two gold-badged ⭐ METHOD PROBLEMS are both complete graphs: one with a horizontal asymptote, one with a slant. Your teacher reads them as six-step arguments — factoring, intercepts, asymptotes, long run, sign chart, assembly — where every feature cites its evidence and the final curve honors all of it. Give every problem honest work, and give the method problems the full procedure with nothing skipped because it felt obvious.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Once the intercepts, asymptotes, and signs are all computed, how much freedom is actually left in the drawing — and what does that say about what a graph is?
📝 PROBLEM SET

Show your work for every problem in neat vertical form — one step per line, equals signs aligned, final answers labeled. Draw graphs large, with asymptotes dashed and labeled. Fractions stay exact.

1
State the horizontal or slant asymptote of each, naming the degree case that decides it:   ① \(\dfrac{4x^2 - 1}{2x^2 + x}\)   ② \(\dfrac{x - 3}{x^2 + 2}\)   ③ \(\dfrac{2x^3}{x^2 + 1}\) (divide for this one).
2
For \(f(x) = \dfrac{3x^2 - 5x + 2}{x^2 - 4}\): find the horizontal asymptote, then determine exactly where (if anywhere) the graph crosses it, by solving \(f(x) = L\). Report the crossing point or prove there is none.
⭐ METHOD PROBLEM — your teacher grades this one for method
3
Produce a complete graph of \(f(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - x - 6}{x^2 - 1}\), following the six-step procedure with every step labeled and shown: ① factored form, domain, holes; ② all intercepts; ③ vertical asymptotes; ④ horizontal asymptote and the exact crossing point if the graph meets it (solve for it — this one does); ⑤ full sign chart with a test value in every interval, arithmetic shown; ⑥ the assembled graph, each branch consistent with the chart and the asymptote approaches. Finish with one sentence naming which feature would be easiest to get wrong by eye and which computation protects it.
4
For \(g(x) = \dfrac{x+2}{(x-1)(x+4)}\): build the sign chart (markers, test values, arithmetic), state the horizontal asymptote, and give the one-sided arrow-notation behavior at both vertical asymptotes, each direction justified from the chart.
⭐ METHOD PROBLEM — your teacher grades this one for method
5
Produce a complete graph of \(h(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - 2x - 3}{x - 2}\): ① perform the division and rewrite \(h\) as line plus leftover, stating the slant asymptote; ② find all intercepts and the vertical asymptote; ③ use the sign of the leftover to determine which side of the slant line each branch rides, showing the reasoning in one or two sentences; ④ determine whether the graph ever crosses its slant asymptote by solving \(h(x) = \text{line}\), and state what the result means; ⑤ assemble the graph with every feature labeled.
6
A classmate begins hunting for the slant asymptote of \(g(x) = \dfrac{x^2 + 3x - 4}{x - 1}\). Show why the hunt is misconceived: factor first, simplify, and describe the actual graph completely (it is startlingly simple, with one subtle feature). State the general moral in one sentence.
7
Honors stretch: Design a rational function meeting all of this specification, and verify each feature from your factored form: vertical asymptotes at \(x = 2\) and \(x = -2\); horizontal asymptote \(y = 3\); exactly one \(x\)-intercept, at \(x = 1\), where the graph touches the axis without crossing. (Multiplicity is your friend on the last clause.)

📊 GRADING RUBRIC

Your teacher applies this rubric to the two ⭐ method problems (the rest of the set is checked for honest completion). This same four-criterion standard governs every hand-graded item in this course, all year.

Criteria Excellent (4) Proficient (3) Developing (2) Beginning (1) Pts
Understanding of the material
4 pts		 Method fits the problem exactly; definitions, notation, and results are used correctly throughout. Method is sound with a minor conceptual slip that does not derail the solution. Partial grasp; the approach starts correctly but loses the thread. The method does not match the mathematics being asked. 4 pts
Quality of reasoning
4 pts		 Every step follows from the last; the why is stated, and verification is actually performed. Reasoning is mostly explicit; one link in the chain is asserted rather than shown. Steps are present but read as recipe-following; explanations restate what without why. Answer appears with little or no supporting reasoning. 4 pts
Presentation & precision
4 pts		 Clean vertical layout, aligned equals signs, labeled answers, exact values; graphs read as visual arguments. Organized and legible with small lapses in labeling or alignment. Readable but disorderly; the grader must hunt for the thread. Work is scattered or illegible; steps cannot be followed. 4 pts
Intellectual honesty
4 pts		 All work shown faithfully as done; uncertainty and gaps are named plainly; nothing is dressed up. Work is genuine; one rough patch is smoothed over rather than acknowledged. Some steps appear reverse-engineered from the answer rather than worked. The page misrepresents how the work was actually done. 4 pts
