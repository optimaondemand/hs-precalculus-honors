📋 BEFORE YOU BEGIN

This test gathers the whole three-week arc into one sitting: end behavior and the shapes of polynomial graphs, division and its two theorems, the complete hunt for zeros, the anatomy of rational functions, and the honest choice between the families. It has three parts: Part A (fluency), Part B (reasoning — write these as arguments), and Part C (one synthesis problem reaching back to Unit 1).

Ground rules. Set aside a quiet block of about 90 minutes and complete the test in one honest sitting. Work alone: no classmates, no AI tools, no solution websites — this is the one place where the mathematics must be entirely yours. A calculator may be used for arithmetic only (evaluating powers and quotients); every setup, division, factorization, and step of reasoning must appear on your page. Lesson pages and notes stay closed — the theorems this test needs are ones you have proved yourself.

Write the honor line at the top of your first page and sign it: “I completed this test on my own, without help from people, notes, or AI tools.” Intellectual honesty is a quarter of the rubric — and in the unit whose virtue is honesty, it is also the point.

📝 PART A — FLUENCY (Lessons 2-1 through 2-10)

Neat vertical work for every problem — one step per line, equals signs aligned, exact values, labeled answers.

1
State the degree, leading coefficient, and end behavior (arrow notation, both directions) of each — the second without expanding:   ① \(f(x) = 7 - 2x^3 + x^2\)   ② \(g(x) = -4(x-1)^2(x+2)(2x+1)\)
2
Sketch \(f(x) = (x+3)(x-1)^2\) by hand: end behavior, each intercept annotated with its multiplicity and behavior (cross / touch / flatten), the \(y\)-intercept, and a curve consistent with all of it.
3
Divide by long division and state the identity dividend = divisor × quotient + remainder: \((x^3 + 2x^2 - 5x + 1) \div (x^2 + x - 1)\).
4
Divide \(f(x) = 2x^4 - 3x^3 + x - 5\) by \(x - 2\) synthetically (mind the placeholder), state the quotient and remainder, and audit the remainder by the Remainder Theorem — both computations shown.
5
Find all zeros of \(f(x) = x^3 - 2x^2 - 5x + 6\) by the full method: candidate list, a successful synthetic division, and the factored finish. Write the complete factorization.
6
Build the degree-3 polynomial with real coefficients having zeros \(2i\) and \(5\), with \(f(0) = -20\). Give it in factored form (real factors) and expanded standard form, and verify against the given point.
7
For \(f(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - 4}{x^2 - 2x - 3}\): state the domain, all \(x\)- and \(y\)-intercepts, every vertical asymptote, and the horizontal asymptote — each feature justified from the factored form.
8
For \(g(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - 5x + 6}{x - 2}\): find the domain, simplify, and describe the graph completely and precisely — including the coordinates of the feature that makes this function not identical to the line it resembles.
📝 PART B — REASONING (write these as arguments)

These problems are graded primarily on the quality of your reasoning. Full sentences where explanation is asked; every claim earned, not asserted.

9
Prove the Remainder Theorem — when \(f(x)\) is divided by \(x - k\), the remainder equals \(f(k)\) — starting from the division algorithm, with every line written and the key substitution explained in a sentence. Then derive the Factor Theorem from it, both directions, as you would for a classmate who has never seen either proof.
10
Solve \(\dfrac{2x}{x+3} = 1 - \dfrac{6}{x+3}\) with the full discipline: forbidden list posted first, the cleared equation solved, every candidate judged. State your conclusion in a full sentence — and then explain, in two or three more sentences, exactly where in the algebra the counterfeit candidate was manufactured (what operation, at what value, and why that operation can create solutions that were never there).
11
Solve \(\dfrac{x-4}{x+1} \ge 2\) by sign chart — zero on one side, one combined fraction, test values shown. Give the answer in interval notation, and justify each endpoint's bracket or parenthesis with its own reason (the two endpoints have different reasons; name both). Close with one sentence on why clearing the denominator was never an option.
📝 PART C — SYNTHESIS (this unit meets Unit 1)
12
In Unit 1 you proved that geometric growth eventually overtakes arithmetic growth. This unit adds the next rung: geometric growth eventually overtakes polynomial growth of any degree. Put the sequences \(a_n = n^3\) and \(b_n = 2^n\) on trial:
① Compute both at \(n = 2, 5, 8, 9, 10\), and identify where \(2^n\) takes the lead for good.
② Explain why the overtaking was inevitable, using the growth-ratio argument: compute what \(\frac{b_{n+1}}{b_n}\) equals for every \(n\), compute what \(\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\) equals in terms of \(n\), and describe what happens to the polynomial's ratio as \(n\) grows. Two or three sentences drawing the conclusion.
③ In a short closing paragraph (3–5 sentences), connect this to the unit's theme of truthful modeling: a biologist is choosing between a cubic and a geometric/exponential model for a bacteria colony's first days of unchecked growth. Which family's long-run testimony fits unchecked growth, what does the cubic eventually understate no matter its coefficients, and what kind of real-world change would make both models dishonest and demand an asymptote instead?
📊 GRADING RUBRIC

The whole test is graded against the standing four-criterion rubric, each criterion scored out of 10. Part A supplies most of the evidence for understanding and precision; Parts B and C supply most of the evidence for reasoning.

Criteria What excellent looks like Pts
Understanding of the material The right tool reaches every problem: end behavior read from leading terms, features from factored forms, candidates judged against forbidden lists, notation exact throughout. 10 pts
Quality of reasoning The proofs in Part B read as real arguments; extraneous candidates are traced to their manufacturing step; verifications are actually performed; the synthesis paragraph reasons about all cases, not one example. 10 pts
Presentation & precision Clean vertical work throughout, aligned equals signs, exact values, labeled sketches with dashed asymptotes, pages in order — forty points of work a stranger could follow. 10 pts
Intellectual honesty Honor line signed and honored; work shown as actually done; any problem you could not finish carries an honest note of where and why you stopped — which earns respect here, never punishment. 10 pts
