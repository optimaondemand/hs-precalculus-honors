📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This is Week 6's Focus assignment and the unit's summation: choosing between the two families and defending the choice like a mathematician. The two gold-badged ⭐ METHOD PROBLEMS are both written defenses in the lesson's four-line form — claim, structural evidence, long-run evidence, domain of honesty — one rejecting a polynomial, one embracing it. Your teacher grades the argument: whether each line carries real evidence and whether the rejected candidate's falsehood is named precisely. Full sentences; the mathematics computed, not gestured at.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Two models can agree everywhere the data lives and still tell opposite stories about the future. On what grounds does an honest mathematician pick a future?
📝 PROBLEM SET

Show your work in neat vertical form. Model defenses are written in the four-line form from the lesson, in full sentences, with all supporting computation shown.

1
For each situation, name the honest family (polynomial or rational) with one structural reason and one long-run reason:   ① the area enclosed by a rectangular pen built from 100 m of fencing, as a function of its width;   ② the average cost per ticket for a concert with a fixed $2,000 venue fee plus $5 per attendee in staffing;   ③ the total fuel cost of a road trip of \(x\) miles at a fixed cost per mile;   ④ the concentration of a single dose of medication in the bloodstream over the day after it is taken.
⭐ METHOD PROBLEM — your teacher grades this one for method
2
A lake contaminated with 12 mg/L of pollutant is being flushed by a steady inflow of clean water. Concentration readings over the first three months fit two candidates about equally well: the line \(L(t) = 12 - 1.8t\) and the rational function \(R(t) = \dfrac{60}{t + 5}\) (both in mg/L, \(t\) in months; check that both give 12 at \(t = 0\)). Write the full four-line model defense for the trustworthy candidate: ① claim; ② structural evidence (what kind of quantity a concentration is, and what the clean inflow does to the long run); ③ long-run evidence — compute each candidate's prediction at \(t = 10\) and \(t = 30\), state each family's end behavior, and name precisely the falsehood the rejected model commits and when it first commits it; ④ domain of honesty for each candidate. Close with one sentence on why the near-tie in fit over three months settles nothing.
3
Using the honest model from problem 2: find when the concentration reaches 2 mg/L (full guard-and-judge discipline), and state — with the asymptote as evidence — whether the lake ever reads exactly 0 mg/L under this model, and what it approaches instead.
⭐ METHOD PROBLEM — your teacher grades this one for method
4
A trophy maker carves bases with a square footprint of side \(s\) inches and a height one inch less than \(s\). ① Build the volume model and write its four-line defense — including why the polynomial's runaway ends are no objection here, and why a rational challenger would have nothing in the situation to model with its denominator. ② A customer orders a base of exactly 48 cubic inches: solve for \(s\) by the full Week-4 method (candidates, synthetic division, quadratic remnant), accounting in writing for every root the algebra produces. ③ Verify the surviving answer in the original situation, with units.
5
A subscription-box company with $5,000 monthly fixed costs and $6 per-box materials fits its average cost over eight months with a line and announces: “average cost is falling $0.90 per month — by year three we'll be under $4 a box.” Correct the announcement: build the honest model, compute its floor, and explain in two or three sentences what the line was actually tracking and where its useful life ends. (The claim of “under $4” violates something specific — name it.)
6
Honors stretch: Some honest situations defeat both of this unit's families. Consider the number of daylight hours in Orlando, tracked daily for several years. Explain in a short paragraph why no polynomial and no rational function can model it honestly — argue from each family's possible long-run behaviors and from what the daylight data actually does forever. Then state, in one sentence, what property the honest model family would need. (You have just written the job description for a function you will meet in Unit 7.)
💡 CHECK YOURSELF WITH DESMOS
Run the lesson's two-window habit on problem 2 in Desmos (desmos.com/calculator): both candidates in the data window (\(0 \le t \le 3\)) to see the near-tie, then the destiny window (\(0 \le t \le 30\)) to watch the line commit its falsehood and the rational curve keep its promise. For problem 4, graph the volume model with \(y = 48\) and confirm your solved \(s\) at the intersection. The calculator confirms; your algebra convinces.

📊 GRADING RUBRIC

Your teacher applies this rubric to the two ⭐ method problems (the rest of the set is checked for honest completion). This same four-criterion standard governs every hand-graded item in this course, all year.

Criteria Excellent (4) Proficient (3) Developing (2) Beginning (1) Pts
Understanding of the material
4 pts		 Method fits the problem exactly; definitions, notation, and results are used correctly throughout. Method is sound with a minor conceptual slip that does not derail the solution. Partial grasp; the approach starts correctly but loses the thread. The method does not match the mathematics being asked. 4 pts
Quality of reasoning
4 pts		 Every step follows from the last; the why is stated, and verification is actually performed. Reasoning is mostly explicit; one link in the chain is asserted rather than shown. Steps are present but read as recipe-following; explanations restate what without why. Answer appears with little or no supporting reasoning. 4 pts
Presentation & precision
4 pts		 Clean vertical layout, aligned equals signs, labeled answers, exact values; prose in complete sentences. Organized and legible with small lapses in labeling or alignment. Readable but disorderly; the grader must hunt for the thread. Work is scattered or illegible; steps cannot be followed. 4 pts
Intellectual honesty
4 pts		 All work shown faithfully as done; uncertainty and gaps are named plainly; nothing is dressed up. Work is genuine; one rough patch is smoothed over rather than acknowledged. Some steps appear reverse-engineered from the answer rather than worked. The page misrepresents how the work was actually done. 4 pts
