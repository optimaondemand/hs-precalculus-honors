📋 BEFORE YOU BEGIN

This test gathers the whole unit into one sitting: components, magnitude and direction, graphical and algebraic addition and subtraction, scalar multiplication, unit vectors, conversions among vector forms, resultants, the dot product, orthogonality, and vector projections. It has three parts: Part A (fluency), Part B (reasoning — write these as arguments), and Part C (one synthesis problem connecting this unit to Unit 9).

Ground rules. Set aside a quiet block of about 90 minutes and complete the test in one honest sitting. Work alone: no classmates, no AI tools, no solution websites — this is the one place where the mathematics must be entirely yours. A calculator may be used for arithmetic only (evaluating a decimal, checking a computation); every setup, every conversion, and every step of written argument must appear on your page — the calculator may check a number, never supply the reasoning. Lesson pages and notes stay closed.

Write the honor line at the top of your first page and sign it: “I completed this test on my own, without help from people, notes, or AI tools.” Intellectual honesty is a quarter of the rubric — and in the unit whose virtue is service, honest work is itself a form of service: to your teacher's trust, and to every classmate whose work is measured against the same honest standard.

📝 PART A — FLUENCY (Lessons 10-1 through 10-13)

Neat vertical work for every problem — one step per line, exact values kept exact, labeled answers.

1
Components, magnitude, direction: A vector runs from point \(A(2,-1)\) to point \(B(7,11)\). Write it in component form, and find its magnitude and direction angle.
2
Addition and subtraction, graphical and algebraic: Let \(\mathbf{u}=\langle-3,7\rangle\) and \(\mathbf{v}=\langle5,-2\rangle\). ① Sketch a tip-to-tail diagram showing \(\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}\), briefly describing where \(\mathbf{v}\)'s tail is placed. ② Confirm your sketch algebraically by computing \(\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}\) and \(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\) in component form.
3
Resultant from magnitudes and directions: Two forces act on an object: \(8\) lb at \(0^\circ\) and \(6\) lb at \(90^\circ\). Find the resultant's component form, magnitude, and direction angle.
4
Scalar multiplication: Let \(\mathbf{v}=\langle-4,9\rangle\). Find \(3\mathbf{v}\) and \(-2\mathbf{v}\), and describe in a sentence how each scalar changes \(\mathbf{v}\)'s magnitude and direction.
5
Unit vectors: Find the unit vector in the direction of \(\mathbf{w}=\langle-8,15\rangle\).
6
Form conversion: Convert \(\mathbf{v}=\langle-5\sqrt3,-5\rangle\) to trigonometric (magnitude-angle) form, showing the magnitude, the reference angle, and your quadrant reasoning.
7
Dot product and orthogonality: Let \(\mathbf{p}=\langle6,-2\rangle\) and \(\mathbf{q}=\langle1,3\rangle\). Compute \(\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{q}\), and state whether \(\mathbf{p}\) and \(\mathbf{q}\) are orthogonal, with your reasoning.
8
Vector projection: Find \(\text{proj}_{\mathbf{b}}\mathbf{a}\) for \(\mathbf{a}=\langle8,1\rangle\) and \(\mathbf{b}=\langle4,3\rangle\), and state the magnitude of the projection vector you found.
📝 PART B — REASONING (write these as arguments)

These problems are graded primarily on the quality of your reasoning. Full sentences where explanation is asked; every claim earned, not asserted.

9
A boat, an engine, and a current. A boat's engine is set for \(24\) mph due east in still water. A current flows due south at \(7\) mph. ① Write both velocity vectors in component form, and find the resultant ground-velocity vector. ② Find the boat's actual speed over the ground and its direction, expressed as an angle south of east. ③ In two or three sentences, explain why vector addition — rather than simply adding the two speeds arithmetically — is the correct way to combine the engine's velocity and the current's velocity.
10
Where the angle formula actually comes from. Let \(\mathbf{u}\) and \(\mathbf{v}\) be two vectors with angle \(\theta\) between them, placed tail-to-tail so that \(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\) is the third side of the triangle they form. ① Apply the Law of Cosines to that triangle to write an expression for \(|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}|^2\) in terms of \(|\mathbf{u}|\), \(|\mathbf{v}|\), and \(\theta\). ② Separately, expand \(|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}|^2=(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v})\cdot(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v})\) using the algebraic properties of the dot product. ③ Set your two expressions for \(|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}|^2\) equal to each other, and show every step of algebra needed to arrive at \(\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}=|\mathbf{u}||\mathbf{v}|\cos\theta\).
📝 PART C — SYNTHESIS (this unit meets Unit 9)
11
Consider the vector \(\mathbf{v}=-3\mathbf{i}+3\sqrt3\,\mathbf{j}\), written in component form as \(\langle-3,3\sqrt3\rangle\).
① Write \(\mathbf{v}\) in vector trigonometric (magnitude-angle) form, showing the magnitude, the reference angle, and your quadrant reasoning explicitly.
② Re-express that same magnitude-angle pair as the complex number \(-3+3\sqrt3\,i\) in trigonometric form, exactly as Unit 9 taught.
③ Verify. Convert your trigonometric form back to rectangular/component form for both the vector and the complex number, using \(x=r\cos\theta\) and \(y=r\sin\theta\), and confirm each one recovers the original exactly.
④ Name the connection. In one or two sentences: why is a vector's trigonometric form and a complex number's trigonometric form the same underlying mathematical object, wearing two different uniforms?
📊 GRADING RUBRIC

The whole test is graded against the standing four-criterion rubric, each criterion scored out of 10. Part A supplies most of the evidence for understanding and precision; Parts B and C supply most of the evidence for reasoning.

Criteria What excellent looks like Pts
Understanding of the material Vector components, magnitude, direction, addition, scalar multiplication, form conversion, the dot product, and projection are all executed correctly and fluently, with notation exact throughout. 10 pts
Quality of reasoning The wind-and-current argument and the Law-of-Cosines derivation both read as genuine arguments; the Part C connection to Unit 9's trigonometric form is made explicitly and correctly, not merely gestured at. 10 pts
Presentation & precision Clean vertical work throughout, aligned equals signs, exact values kept in radical or degree form where called for, pages in order — forty points of work a stranger could follow. 10 pts
Intellectual honesty Honor line signed and honored; work shown as actually done; any problem you could not finish carries an honest note of where and why you stopped — which earns respect here, never punishment. 10 pts
