📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This is Week 13's Focus assignment — the one set this week your teacher grades for method, and the last assignment before Thursday's unit test. The two gold-badged ⭐ METHOD PROBLEMS are the unit's two crowning moves: a complete inverse construction with guards posted, the fork resolved, and both compositions proved; and the formal closing of the Unit 3 loop — an exponential model whose logarithmic inverse you must build by swap-and-solve, use, and verify. Your teacher reads them as arguments: every guard with its reason, every composition actually computed, every inverse answer judged by the forward function, verdicts in sentences. Give every problem honest work, and give the method problems the full procedure with nothing skipped because it felt obvious.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
A question that hands you the output and demands the input cannot be answered by the forward function — yet the forward function is the judge of every answer. How does an inverse get built, put to work, and proven right?
📝 PROBLEM SET

Show your work for every problem in neat vertical form — one step per line, equals signs aligned, final answers labeled with units. Every inverse answer judged by one substitution into the forward function. Exact values throughout.

1
Both directions of a fare: A taxi charges a \$2.50 flag drop plus \$3.20 per mile, so \(C(m) = 3.2m + 2.5\). ① Forward: what does a 7-mile ride cost? ② Construct the inverse \(m(C)\). ③ Inverse: how far can you ride on \$18.50? Judge the answer with the forward function, and label which of the two questions was the inverse problem and how you knew.
⭐ METHOD PROBLEM — your teacher grades this one for method
2
Complete inverse construction for \(f(x) = \sqrt{3x - 6} + 1\), every step labeled: ① state the domain and range of \(f\), each with its reason (radicand guard; principal-root outputs, lifted); ② swap and solve — post the guard on the new variable before squaring, and show every algebraic step to \(f^{-1}\); ③ state \(f^{-1}\) with its domain, and confirm that domain equals the range of \(f\); ④ prove the inverse by composition in both directions, arithmetic shown — and in the direction where \(\sqrt{(x-1)^2}\) appears, name explicitly why the absolute value may be dropped; ⑤ verdict sentence: state what the two compositions together certify that a single point-check could not.
3
Decode the transmission: A classmate encodes letters (A \(= 1\) through Z \(= 26\)) with \(E(n) = 4n - 1\) and sends you 59, 91, 47, 75. Construct the decoding function \(E^{-1}\), decode the message, and judge each letter by re-encoding it. Then answer in one sentence: why would the cipher \(E(n) = (n - 13)^2\) be useless, no matter how clever the sender? (Name the property that fails and the two letters that collide.)
⭐ METHOD PROBLEM — your teacher grades this one for method
4
Closing the Unit 3 loop: A patient's bloodstream holds \(A(t) = 200 \cdot \left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{t/6}\) milligrams of caffeine \(t\) hours after a strong coffee — a half-life of 6 hours. ① Forward: how much remains after 12 hours? ② Construct the inverse by swap-and-solve, showing every step — including the rewriting \(\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{t/6} = 2^{-t/6}\) (or an equivalent route) and the precise moment the logarithm enters as the name of the inverse question; state \(t(A)\) cleanly. ③ Inverse: the patient can sleep once caffeine drops to 25 mg — find that time exactly, then judge it with the forward function, arithmetic shown. ④ Verdict paragraph (2–3 sentences): name the question the forward function could not answer directly, and state what this construction proves about the logarithm that Unit 3 asked you to accept on authority — the loop, closed in your own words.
5
Audit the AI: A student asked an AI: “A colony obeys \(P(t) = 300 \cdot 2^{t/4}\). When does it reach 4800?” It replied: “Taking logs: \(\frac{t}{4} = \frac{\log_2 4800}{\log_2 300} \approx \frac{12.23}{8.23} \approx 1.49\), so \(t \approx 5.9\) hours.” Write the examiner's report: ① judge the AI's answer with the forward function — compute \(P(5.9)\) approximately and state the verdict; ② solve the equation correctly by the lesson's method (isolate the growth factor first), exactly, and judge your own answer the same way; ③ name the corrupted property in one precise sentence — what did the AI claim \(\log_2\!\big(\frac{4800}{300}\big)\) equals, and what does it actually equal? ④ one closing sentence connecting this failure to the checkpoint pattern you have now seen three times in this unit.
6
Socks and shoes: Let \(f(x) = 2x + 1\) and \(g(x) = x - 3\). ① Compute \((f \circ g)(x)\) and find its inverse directly. ② Compute \(g^{-1}\!\left(f^{-1}(x)\right)\) and \(f^{-1}\!\left(g^{-1}(x)\right)\), and identify which one matches the direct inverse. ③ Explain the winner in one sentence: when a two-step process is undone, why must the last step be undone first?
7
Explain why: In three or four sentences: why is being one-to-one exactly the condition that makes a function invertible — and decodable? Your explanation should describe what goes wrong with the reverse question at a repeated output, and should end with one sentence on what this unit added to Unit 3's logarithm: what is the difference between accepting “log is the inverse of the exponential” as a title and deriving it as a theorem?
8
Honors stretch: Show that every function of the form \(f(x) = \dfrac{ax + b}{x - a}\) (with \(a^2 + b \neq 0\)) is its own inverse: compute \(f\!\left(f(x)\right)\) in general — careful, patient algebra with the constants — and simplify to \(x\), noting where the condition \(a^2 + b \neq 0\) is needed. Confirm your general result concretely with \(a = 2\), \(b = 1\): compute \(f(3)\), then \(f\) of the result, and watch 3 come home. Close with one sentence: what does self-inverse mean for the graph's relationship to the line \(y = x\), and where in this week's work have you met a self-inverse function before?

📊 GRADING RUBRIC

Your teacher applies this rubric to the two ⭐ method problems (the rest of the set is checked for honest completion). This same four-criterion standard governs every hand-graded item in this course, all year.

Criteria Excellent (4) Proficient (3) Developing (2) Beginning (1) Pts
Understanding of the material
4 pts		 Method fits the problem exactly; definitions, notation, and results are used correctly throughout. Method is sound with a minor conceptual slip that does not derail the solution. Partial grasp; the approach starts correctly but loses the thread. The method does not match the mathematics being asked. 4 pts
Quality of reasoning
4 pts		 Every step follows from the last; the why is stated, and verification is actually performed. Reasoning is mostly explicit; one link in the chain is asserted rather than shown. Steps are present but read as recipe-following; explanations restate what without why. Answer appears with little or no supporting reasoning. 4 pts
Presentation & precision
4 pts		 Clean vertical layout, aligned equals signs, labeled answers with units, exact values; work reads as a visual argument. Organized and legible with small lapses in labeling or alignment. Readable but disorderly; the grader must hunt for the thread. Work is scattered or illegible; steps cannot be followed. 4 pts
Intellectual honesty
4 pts		 All work shown faithfully as done; uncertainty and gaps are named plainly; nothing is dressed up. Work is genuine; one rough patch is smoothed over rather than acknowledged. Some steps appear reverse-engineered from the answer rather than worked. The page misrepresents how the work was actually done. 4 pts
