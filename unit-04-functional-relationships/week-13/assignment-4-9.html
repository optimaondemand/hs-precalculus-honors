Practice Set: In the Mirror of y = x
Assignment · 10 pts
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This set drills the whole of Lesson 4-9: inverses found by swap-and-solve and certified by composition, graphs mirrored across the identity line with swapped anchor points, domains and ranges exchanged down to the asymptotes, and inverse values read straight out of a table. This is a practice set: scored for honest completion, with worked solutions released after the due date for line-by-line self-correction.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
One coordinate swap gives the algebra, the graph, and the bookkeeping of every inverse. Where does each consequence come from — and how does the composition check certify them all?
📝 PROBLEM SET

Show your work for every problem in neat vertical form — one step per line, equals signs aligned, final answers labeled. Draw graphs large, with anchor points labeled and the line \(y = x\) drawn dashed. Exact values throughout.

1
Swap-and-solve drill: Find the inverse of \(f(x) = 3x - 5\), then verify it by both compositions, arithmetic shown: \(f\!\left(f^{-1}(x)\right) = x\) and \(f^{-1}\!\left(f(x)\right) = x\).
2
Odd powers need no guard: Find the inverse of \(g(x) = x^3 - 4\), verify with the point check \(g(2) = 4\), and answer in one sentence: why did undoing the cube raise no \(\pm\) fork and no branch worry? (Lesson 3-10's parity principle is the key.)
3
A surprise in the mirror: Find the inverse of \(f(x) = \dfrac{x + 2}{x - 1}\) by swap-and-solve. State what you notice about your answer — then confirm the surprise by computing \(f(f(x))\) and simplifying completely. What must the graph of this function look like relative to the line \(y = x\)?
4
Read the table backward: The table \(\begin{array}{c|ccccc} x & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 \\ \hline f(x) & 5 & 2 & -1 & -4 & -7 \end{array}\) defines a one-to-one function. Find \(f^{-1}(2)\), \(f^{-1}(-7)\), and \(\left(f^{-1} \circ f\right)(3)\) — and write out the full table of \(f^{-1}\).
5
The mirror, drawn: Find the inverse of \(f(x) = 2x - 4\), then hand-graph \(f\), \(f^{-1}\), and the dashed line \(y = x\) on one grid. Label both intercepts of each line — and state in a phrase how the intercepts of \(f\) and \(f^{-1}\) are related, and why.
6
Guard the reversal: Find the inverse of \(f(x) = \sqrt{x - 3}\), posting the guard before you square. Give the inverse with its domain, state the domain and range of both functions, and verify \(f\!\left(f^{-1}(x)\right) = x\) — naming the exact step where the guard makes \(\sqrt{x^2} = x\) legitimate.
7
Explain why: In three or four sentences, explain why the graph of \(f^{-1}\) must be the reflection of the graph of \(f\) across the line \(y = x\). Your explanation should start from the single fact that \(f(a) = b\) forces \(f^{-1}(b) = a\), and should say what reflection across \(y = x\) does to the coordinates of a point. (The lesson's blue box, retold in your voice — not copied.)
8
Honors stretch: Find the inverse of \(f(x) = \dfrac{2x + 1}{x - 3}\). Then put the Inverse Dictionary on display: state the vertical and horizontal asymptotes of \(f\) and of \(f^{-1}\), show that they have traded places, and explain in two sentences why the trade is forced — what does a vertical asymptote say about the domain, what does a horizontal asymptote say about the range, and what does an inverse do to domain and range? Finish with one point check in each direction.
💡 CHECK YOURSELF WITH DESMOS
After solving on paper, verify problems 3, 5, 6, and 8 in Desmos (desmos.com/calculator) with the lesson's trick: graph the original as y = f(x), its inverse as the swapped relation x = f(y), and your algebraic answer as a third equation — your formula should land exactly on the swapped relation. Add dashed y = x and check the mirror symmetry by eye. For problem 3, all the curves should coincide — the surprise made visible. The calculator confirms; your algebra convinces.
