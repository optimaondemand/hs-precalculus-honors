📋 BEFORE YOU BEGIN

This test gathers the whole three-week arc into one sitting: rates of change and the difference quotient, the arithmetic and composition of functions with their negotiated domains, and inverses — found, restricted, graphed, and put to work. It has three parts: Part A (fluency), Part B (reasoning — write these as arguments), and Part C (one synthesis problem that closes the loop with Unit 3).

Ground rules. Set aside a quiet block of about 90 minutes and complete the test in one honest sitting. Work alone: no classmates, no AI tools, no solution websites — this is the one place where the mathematics must be entirely yours. A calculator may be used for arithmetic only (evaluating powers and quotients); every setup, expansion, composition, and step of reasoning must appear on your page. Lesson pages and notes stay closed — the methods this test needs are ones you have practiced all unit.

Write the honor line at the top of your first page and sign it: “I completed this test on my own, without help from people, notes, or AI tools.” Intellectual honesty is a quarter of the rubric — and in the unit whose virtue is courtesy, honest work is the first courtesy you owe your reader.

📝 PART A — FLUENCY (Lessons 4-1 through 4-10)

Neat vertical work for every problem — one step per line, equals signs aligned, exact values, labeled answers.

1
Compute the average rate of change of \(f(x) = x^2 - 3x\) over the interval \([2, 6]\), work shown — then state in one sentence what the number means about the graph of \(f\) between those two inputs.
2
Write and completely simplify the difference quotient \(\dfrac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}\) for \(f(x) = 5x - x^2\) — expansion shown, the \(h\) cancelled honestly (not struck out of an unfactored sum).
3
For \(f(x) = \sqrt{x - 1}\) and \(g(x) = x^2 - 4\): evaluate \((f + g)(5)\) and \((fg)(2)\), and state the domain of \(\left(\dfrac{f}{g}\right)(x)\) — every excluded or required value justified.
4
For \(f(x) = 2x - 1\) and \(g(x) = x^2 + 3\): find \((f \circ g)(x)\) and \((g \circ f)(x)\) as simplified formulas, evaluate each at \(x = 2\), and state in one sentence what the two different results demonstrate about composition.
5
For \(f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x - 2}\) and \(g(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}\): find \((f \circ g)(x)\), simplify it to a single fraction, and state its full domain — including the restriction that the simplified formula no longer shows, with a sentence naming where that hidden restriction comes from.
6
The table \(\begin{array}{c|ccccc} x & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 \\ \hline f(x) & 3 & 5 & 1 & 2 & 4 \\ g(x) & 2 & 0 & 4 & 1 & 3 \end{array}\) defines two functions, with \(f\) one-to-one. Evaluate \((g \circ f)(2)\), \(\;f^{-1}(4)\), \(\;(f \circ f)(1)\), and \(\left(f^{-1} \circ g\right)(3)\) — each with a phrase of justification.
7
Find the inverse of \(f(x) = \dfrac{4x - 1}{x + 2}\) by swap-and-solve, every step shown. Then state the vertical and horizontal asymptotes of both \(f\) and \(f^{-1}\), and note in one sentence the exchange that had to happen and why.
8
Find the inverse of \(f(x) = \sqrt{x + 2}\) with the guard posted before squaring, and hand-graph \(f\), \(f^{-1}\), and the dashed line \(y = x\) on one grid — at least three labeled anchor points on each curve, coordinates visibly swapped, domains of both functions stated.
📝 PART B — REASONING (write these as arguments)

These problems are graded primarily on the quality of your reasoning. Full sentences where explanation is asked; every claim earned, not asserted.

9
The doorstep of the calculus: For \(f(x) = x^2 - 4x + 1\): ① write and completely simplify the difference quotient, every line of algebra shown; ② interpret the simplified expression — what does it measure on the graph of \(f\), between which two points? ③ using your formula, compute the secant slope at \(x = 3\) with \(h = 0.1\); ④ in two or three closing sentences, describe what your expression approaches as \(h\) shrinks toward 0, what the limiting value means at \(x = 3\), and why Leibniz and his century cared so much about exactly this question.
10
Prove two functions are inverses: Let \(f(x) = \dfrac{x - 1}{x - 3}\) and \(g(x) = \dfrac{3x - 1}{x - 1}\). Prove that \(g = f^{-1}\) by computing both compositions \(f\!\left(g(x)\right)\) and \(g\!\left(f(x)\right)\) and simplifying each to \(x\) — every algebraic step written, no step skipped as obvious. State the domain of each function, and close with two sentences: why does the definition demand both directions, and what do the asymptotes of \(f\) and \(g\) — read from the formulas — already predict about the pair?
11
Restriction as argument: Consider \(f(x) = x^2 - 6x + 11\). ① Show that \(f\) has no inverse on its full domain, exhibiting a witness pair of inputs that share an output, arithmetic shown. ② Complete the square to locate the fold, and restrict \(f\) to its right branch. ③ Find the inverse of the restricted function — the \(\pm\) fork resolved in writing against the declared branch — and state the inverse's domain with its justification. ④ Verify with the point \(f(5) = 6\). ⑤ Close with one sentence naming the spring horizon: what family of functions, arriving in Unit 7, will owe its existence to exactly the move you just made?
📝 PART C — SYNTHESIS (this unit meets Unit 3)
12
In Unit 3 you solved exponential equations by “taking the log of both sides” — a procedure justified by a promise: the logarithm is the inverse of the exponential. This unit built the machinery behind the promise. Now close the loop yourself. An investment account holds \(V(t) = 4000 \cdot 2^{t/9}\) dollars after \(t\) years — the balance doubles every 9 years.
① Forward: compute the balance after 18 years, work shown.
② Construct the inverse \(t(V)\) by swap-and-solve, every step written — and mark the exact line where the logarithm enters as the answer to the question “to what exponent?”
③ Inverse: the account owner is waiting for the balance to reach \$128,000 — a question the forward function cannot answer directly. Answer it exactly with your inverse, then judge the answer by substituting it back into the forward function, arithmetic shown.
④ The loop, closed (3–5 sentences): explain what your construction in ② proves about the logarithm that Unit 3 asked you to take on authority. Your paragraph should name the reverse question the logarithm answers, connect \(\log_2\!\left(2^x\right) = x\) to this unit's composition test for inverses, and say why the exponential — unlike the quadratic in Part B — needed no domain restriction before its inverse could exist.
📊 GRADING RUBRIC

The whole test is graded against the standing four-criterion rubric, each criterion scored out of 10. Part A supplies most of the evidence for understanding and precision; Parts B and C supply most of the evidence for reasoning.

Criteria What excellent looks like Pts
Understanding of the material The right tool reaches every problem: difference quotients expanded before cancelling, composite domains caught at the inner function, forks resolved by declared branches, \(f^{-1}\) never confused with a reciprocal, notation exact throughout. 10 pts
Quality of reasoning The proof in Part B runs both compositions to completion; witness pairs are exhibited, not asserted; every inverse answer is judged against the forward function; the synthesis paragraph closes the loop in the student's own words, not the lesson's. 10 pts
Presentation & precision Clean vertical work throughout, aligned equals signs, exact values, every inverse wearing its domain, graphs with labeled anchor points and a dashed identity line, pages in order — forty points of work a stranger could follow. 10 pts
Intellectual honesty Honor line signed and honored; work shown as actually done; any problem you could not finish carries an honest note of where and why you stopped — which earns respect here, never punishment. 10 pts
Total: 40 pts
