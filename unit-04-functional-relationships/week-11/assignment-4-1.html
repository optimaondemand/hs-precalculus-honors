📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This set drills Lesson 4-1's two sharpened blades: domains read honestly from formulas, and average rates of change computed from formulas, tables, and graphs — with units attached and signs interpreted, every time. This is a practice set: scored for honest completion, with worked solutions released after the due date for line-by-line self-correction.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
A function's formula, table, and graph are three portraits of one subject. What single computation measures its speed of change in all three — and what must always ride along with the answer?
📝 PROBLEM SET

Show your work for every problem in neat vertical form — one step per line, equals signs aligned, final answers labeled with units where the context has them. Keep the order of subtraction consistent, upstairs and down.

1
Domain drills: State the domain of each in interval notation, naming which part of the formula posts each restriction:   ① \(y = \sqrt{5 - x}\)   ② \(y = \dfrac{x + 2}{x^2 - 16}\)   ③ \(y = \dfrac{\sqrt{x - 1}}{x - 6}\).
2
Rates from formulas: Compute the average rate of change of each function on the given interval, evaluating both endpoints first:   ① \(f(x) = x^2 + 2x\) on \([1, 4]\)   ② \(g(x) = \sqrt{x}\) on \([1, 9]\)   ③ \(k(x) = 2^x\) on \([1, 4]\).
3
Rates from a table: A family's road-trip odometer readings: \(\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c|c} t \text{ (hr)} & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 \\ \hline d \text{ (mi)} & 0 & 62 & 130 & 180 & 252 \end{array}\)   Compute the average speed (with units) on \([0, 2]\) and on \([2, 4]\), then find which single hour of the trip was fastest, showing the rate for each hour. What real-world event might the hour from \(t = 2\) to \(t = 3\) suggest?
4
Rate from a graph: A graphed function passes through the points \((-2, 7)\) and \((2, -1)\). Find its average rate of change on \([-2, 2]\), and sketch the two points with the line segment joining them, labeling the rise and the run on your sketch.
5
The race, revisited: Compute the average rate of change of \(f(x) = 2^x\) and of \(g(x) = x^2\) on \([1, 3]\), then on \([3, 5]\). Which function changes faster on each interval? In one sentence, connect the reversal to a truth about exponential growth you established in Units 2 and 3.
6
Explain why: Prove the lesson's claim that a linear function \(f(x) = mx + b\) has the same average rate of change on every interval: compute \(\dfrac{f(q) - f(p)}{q - p}\) for arbitrary inputs \(p \neq q\), simplify completely, and state in a sentence why the answer's form settles the claim.
7
Honors stretch: ① Find the value of \(c > 1\) for which the average rate of change of \(f(x) = x^2\) on \([1, c]\) equals exactly 5. (Simplify \(\frac{c^2 - 1}{c - 1}\) by factoring before solving — the fraction collapses beautifully.) ② Generalize: show that the average rate of change of \(x^2\) on any interval \([a, b]\) is simply \(a + b\). ③ One sentence: your simplification cancelled the factor \((c - 1)\) — where else this week is precalculus about to cancel a “gap” factor just like it, and why does that make this problem a preview?
💡 CHECK YOURSELF WITH EXCEL
After solving on paper, rebuild problems 3 and 5 in the rate-of-change machine from the lesson: inputs in column A, outputs in column B (type the odometer readings, or a formula like =2^A2), and =(B3-B2)/(A3-A2) filled down column C. Excel's column C should reproduce your hand-computed rates exactly — including the reversal in problem 5. If a row disagrees, one of you subtracted in mismatched order; find out which. The spreadsheet confirms; your algebra convinces.
