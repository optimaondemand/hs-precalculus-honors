📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This set drills the protocol of composition from Lesson 4-5: evaluations that run the relay inside-out, composite formulas built and then certified at a trusted point, table compositions, and the decomposition skill that calculus will one day pay you for. This is a practice set: scored for honest completion, with worked solutions released after the due date for line-by-line self-correction.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
In \(f(g(x))\), the function written last is heard first. What exactly travels from \(g\) to \(f\) — and why does reversing the order usually change everything?
📝 PROBLEM SET

Show your work for every problem in neat vertical form — one step per line, equals signs aligned, final answers labeled. For every evaluation, write the relay: the inner value first, then the outer.

1
Relay drills: For \(f(x) = 2x - 5\) and \(g(x) = x^2 + 3\), evaluate, showing the inner result before the outer:   ① \((f \circ g)(2)\)   ② \((g \circ f)(2)\)   ③ \((f \circ f)(0)\)   ④ \((g \circ g)(1)\).
2
Build the formulas: For the same \(f\) and \(g\), build and fully simplify \((f \circ g)(x)\) and \((g \circ f)(x)\). Then certify each formula by checking it at \(x = 2\) against your answers to problem 1 — write the check, not just a check mark.
3
Table relay: Using the tables \(\begin{array}{c|ccccc} x & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 \\ \hline f(x) & 3 & 0 & 4 & 1 & 2 \end{array}\) and \(\begin{array}{c|ccccc} x & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 \\ \hline g(x) & 2 & 4 & 1 & 3 & 0 \end{array}\), evaluate:   ① \((f \circ g)(1)\)   ② \((g \circ f)(0)\)   ③ \((f \circ g)(2)\)   ④ \((g \circ g)(4)\)   ⑤ \((f \circ f)(2)\). Write each baton pass.
4
Decompose: Write each as \(f \circ g\) with neither function equal to the original:   ① \(h(x) = (2x + 1)^5\)   ② \(k(x) = \sqrt{x^2 + 9}\)   ③ \(m(x) = \dfrac{1}{x - 6}\). Then give a second, genuinely different decomposition of \(h\), and verify it composes back to \(h\).
5
A door swings shut: For \(f(x) = \sqrt{x}\) and \(g(x) = x - 3\), evaluate \((f \circ g)(7)\) and \((g \circ f)(9)\) — then attempt \((f \circ g)(1)\). One of the three cannot be computed: say precisely where the relay breaks down and why. (Tomorrow's lesson lives inside your answer.)
6
Explain why: In three or four sentences, explain why \((f \circ g)(x)\) and \((g \circ f)(x)\) are generally different functions, using your problem 2 results as the exhibit. Your explanation should say what the notation's order convention means (which function acts first, and how you can tell) and should name composition as sequencing rather than arithmetic.
7
Honors stretch: Find every linear function \(f(x) = ax + b\) such that \((f \circ f)(x) = 4x + 9\). (Compose \(f\) with itself in general, match coefficients, and hunt down all cases — there are more than one. Verify each survivor by composing it with itself.)
💡 CHECK YOURSELF WITH DESMOS
After solving on paper, open Desmos (desmos.com/calculator), define f(x) = 2x - 5 and g(x) = x^2 + 3, and type f(g(2)) on its own line — Desmos evaluates the relay for you. Check all four parts of problem 1 this way, then graph y = f(g(x)) alongside your simplified formula from problem 2: if the two graphs sit exactly on top of each other, your algebra is certified. The calculator confirms; your algebra convinces.
