📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This is Week 12's Focus assignment — the one set this week your teacher grades for method. The two gold-badged ⭐ METHOD PROBLEMS are the heart of the inverse discipline: one full verification ceremony performed in both orders with the reasoning narrated, and one trial of a false claimant — a candidate that almost undoes, convicted by composition and replaced by the true inverse. Your teacher reads them as arguments: every composition written out step by step, every collapse to \(x\) earned line by line, every verdict stated in a sentence. Give every problem honest work, and give the method problems the full ceremony with nothing waved through because it felt obvious.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Anyone can claim two functions undo each other. What must be shown — in full, in both orders — before the claim deserves the name inverse?
📝 PROBLEM SET

Show your work for every problem in neat vertical form — one step per line, equals signs aligned, final answers labeled. Every verification is two compositions; write both, every time, and end each with a verdict sentence.

1
Warm-up ceremonies: ① Verify that \(f(x) = x - 9\) and \(g(x) = x + 9\) are inverses of each other — both compositions. ② Show that \(f(x) = 6 - x\) is self-inverse, and explain in one phrase why a single composition settles it here.
2
The swap: A one-to-one function \(f\) satisfies \(f(3) = 11\), \(f(-2) = 5\), and \(f(0) = -4\). State \(f^{-1}(11)\), \(f^{-1}(5)\), and \(f^{-1}(-4)\). Then: if \(f^{-1}(7) = 6\), what is \(f(6)\)? One sentence: what happens to the domain and range of \(f\) when you pass to \(f^{-1}\)?
⭐ METHOD PROBLEM — your teacher grades this one for method
3
Perform the complete verification ceremony for \(f(x) = 4x - 7\) and \(g(x) = \dfrac{x + 7}{4}\): ① narrate the undoing — describe \(f\) as its sequence of steps in words, then describe \(g\)'s steps, and state the reverse-order (socks-and-shoes) principle that connects them; ② compute \(f(g(x))\) with every algebraic step shown, down to \(x\); ③ compute \(g(f(x))\) the same way; ④ verdict sentence — state the conclusion, name the identity function, and explain why the definition demands both orders rather than one.
4
An odd-index pair: Verify that \(f(x) = x^3 - 5\) and \(g(x) = \sqrt[3]{x + 5}\) are inverses of each other — both compositions, every step. At the step \(\sqrt[3]{x^3} = x\), add one phrase justifying it by the parity of the index (Unit 3 is watching).
⭐ METHOD PROBLEM — your teacher grades this one for method
5
The false claimant: A student claims \(k(x) = \dfrac{x}{5} - 10\) is the inverse of \(h(x) = 5x + 10\). ① Put the claim on trial: compute both compositions \(k(h(x))\) and \(h(k(x))\) in full, and state the verdict in a sentence — with the evidence (neither composition is the identity). ② Diagnose the failure: describe \(h\)'s steps in words and explain, by the reverse-order principle, exactly what the claimant did in the wrong order. ③ Repair: reason out the true undoing steps in reverse order and write the candidate \(h^{-1}(x)\). ④ Re-run the full ceremony on your repaired candidate — both compositions, every step, verdict sentence. The original claim was settled by composition, and so is yours: the ceremony is the court.
6
The pair you already knew: Evaluate \(\log_3\!\left(3^7\right)\) and \(10^{\log_{10} 4}\) — no calculator, one line each. Then, in two sentences, explain what these Unit 3 identities were secretly certifying all along, naming the inverse pair involved — and note the caveat: \(10^{\log_{10} x} = x\) only for \(x > 0\), and say which territory-trade that caveat comes from. (This thread is picked up for good in Lesson 4-11 next week.)
7
Explain why: In three or four sentences, explain why \(f^{-1}(x)\) does not mean \(\dfrac{1}{f(x)}\) — what Herschel's exponent counts, and what the reciprocal fails. Prove the failure concretely: for \(f(x) = x + 3\), show that composing \(f\) with \(\frac{1}{f(x)}\) does not produce \(x\), then state the true inverse and the one-line ceremony that certifies it.
8
Honors stretch — the half-undone pair: Let \(f(x) = x^2\) and \(g(x) = \sqrt{x}\). ① Show that \(f(g(x)) = x\) for every \(x \geq 0\) — one direction of the ceremony passes. ② Now compute \(g(f(x))\) carefully and show it equals \(|x|\), not \(x\); exhibit the failure numerically at \(x = -3\). ③ Verdict: is \(g\) the inverse of \(f\)? ④ Diagnose in two sentences which property of \(f\) makes any true inverse impossible (consider that \(f(3) = f(-3)\) — where could an undoing function send 9?), and predict what next week's repair will have to do to \(f\) before an inverse can exist.
💡 CHECK YOURSELF WITH DESMOS
After both method problems are done on paper, verify them in Desmos (desmos.com/calculator) exactly as the lesson taught: define each pair, graph y = f(g(x)) and y = g(f(x)), and look for the 45° identity line — the signature of mutual undoing. For problem 5, graph the false claimant's compositions first and see the parallel near-misses (\(y = x - 8\) and \(y = x - 40\)), then graph your repaired inverse's compositions and watch both land exactly on \(y = x\). See the miss on screen; convict it on paper. The calculator confirms; your algebra convinces.

📊 GRADING RUBRIC

Your teacher applies this rubric to the two ⭐ method problems (the rest of the set is checked for honest completion). This same four-criterion standard governs every hand-graded item in this course, all year.

Criteria Excellent (4) Proficient (3) Developing (2) Beginning (1) Pts
Understanding of the material
4 pts		 Method fits the problem exactly; definitions, notation, and results are used correctly throughout. Method is sound with a minor conceptual slip that does not derail the solution. Partial grasp; the approach starts correctly but loses the thread. The method does not match the mathematics being asked. 4 pts
Quality of reasoning
4 pts		 Every step follows from the last; the why is stated, and verification is actually performed. Reasoning is mostly explicit; one link in the chain is asserted rather than shown. Steps are present but read as recipe-following; explanations restate what without why. Answer appears with little or no supporting reasoning. 4 pts
Presentation & precision
4 pts		 Clean vertical layout, aligned equals signs, labeled answers, exact values; graphs read as visual arguments. Organized and legible with small lapses in labeling or alignment. Readable but disorderly; the grader must hunt for the thread. Work is scattered or illegible; steps cannot be followed. 4 pts
Intellectual honesty
4 pts		 All work shown faithfully as done; uncertainty and gaps are named plainly; nothing is dressed up. Work is genuine; one rough patch is smoothed over rather than acknowledged. Some steps appear reverse-engineered from the answer rather than worked. The page misrepresents how the work was actually done. 4 pts
