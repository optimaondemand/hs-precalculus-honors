Review Activity: Mixed Set & the Function Family Field Guide
Assignment · 16 pts · Focus-style (teacher-graded on the two method problems)
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This is finals week's review activity — your dress rehearsal for the Semester 1 final, in two parts. Part A is a mixed problem set that ranges across all five units with no unit labels attached: recognizing which tool a problem wants is itself the skill being rehearsed. Part B is the one-page, hand-drawn Function Family Field Guide — the semester's families gathered onto a single sheet you will wish you could bring to the final (you cannot; making it puts it where you can bring it). The two gold-badged ⭐ METHOD PROBLEMS are the ones your teacher grades for method: one guard-and-judge trial from Unit 3's discipline, and one full inverse construction proved by composition. Give every problem honest work, and give the method problems the complete procedure with nothing skipped because it felt obvious.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Stripped of its unit label, how does a problem tell you which of the semester's tools it needs — and which of the standing disciplines must ride along with that tool?
📝 PART A — MIXED PROBLEM SET (all five units, no labels)

Show your work for every problem in neat vertical form — one step per line, equals signs aligned, final answers labeled. Draw graphs large, with anchor points labeled. Exact values before decimals, throughout.

1
Two sums: ① Evaluate \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{40}(3k + 1)\), naming the family and the formula before using it. ② Decide whether \(18 + 12 + 8 + \cdots\) converges; if it does, check the license and compute the exact sum.
2
The full zero hunt: Find all zeros of \(f(x) = x^3 - 4x^2 + x + 6\) by the complete method — candidate list, a successful synthetic division shown, the factored finish — and write the complete factorization.
3
Rational anatomy: For \(f(x) = \dfrac{2x^2 - 8}{x^2 - x - 6}\): factor completely, then state the domain, the coordinates of the hole, every vertical asymptote, the horizontal asymptote, and both intercepts — each feature traced to its factor. (One removable ghost and one true wall live in this denominator; tell them apart.)
⭐ METHOD PROBLEM — your teacher grades this one for method
4
Solve \(\log_2 x + \log_2(x - 6) = 4\) with the complete guard-and-judge discipline, every step labeled: ① post the guards — every domain condition with its reason, combined into one final guard; ② solve — condense, convert to exponential form, and solve the quadratic with every factoring step shown; ③ judge each candidate against the original equation, arithmetic shown; ④ verdict sentence — name the extraneous candidate and state precisely where it was manufactured: which step enlarged the equation's domain, and why does the ghost satisfy the condensed equation but not the original?
5
Same growth, two languages: A car bought for \$32,000 loses 25% of its value each year. ① Write the year-end values for the first four years and show they form a geometric sequence, naming \(r\). ② Write the continuous model \(V(t)\) and confirm \(V(4)\) matches your fourth year-end value exactly. ③ Find when the car is worth half its purchase price — exact form first (a ratio of logarithms), then a decimal to two places.
⭐ METHOD PROBLEM — your teacher grades this one for method
6
Complete inverse construction for \(f(x) = \sqrt{x - 2} + 3\): ① state the domain and range of \(f\), each justified; ② find \(f^{-1}\) by swap-and-solve, posting the guard before you square and resolving it in writing; ③ state the domain and range of \(f^{-1}\) and note the swap; ④ prove the pair by computing both compositions \(f\!\left(f^{-1}(x)\right)\) and \(f^{-1}\!\left(f(x)\right)\) and simplifying each to \(x\) — every algebraic step written, the absolute-value moment handled honestly against the declared domain; ⑤ verify with the point \(f(6) = 5\), both directions; ⑥ one closing sentence: which Unit 3 function is defined as an inverse in exactly this way?
7
The master key: Classify and convert \(4x^2 + y^2 + 8x - 6y - 3 = 0\) to standard form by completing the square — both squares shown in full — then state the center, vertices, foci, and eccentricity of the resulting conic, each with the computation that produced it.
8
Explain why: In three or four sentences, explain why a geometric sequence is an exponential function sampled at the counting numbers. Write \(a_n = a_1 r^{\,n-1}\) and \(f(x) = a b^x\) side by side, state exactly how \(r\) and \(b\) correspond, and name one question the continuous function can answer that the sequence cannot. (The semester's headline thread, retold in your voice — not copied from the review lesson.)
9
Honors stretch (three units at once): For which values of \(k\) does the line \(y = x + k\) meet the ellipse \(\dfrac{x^2}{8} + \dfrac{y^2}{2} = 1\) in exactly one point? Substitute, clear the fractions, and let the discriminant of the resulting quadratic carry the argument — all three cases (two meetings, one, none) told as an inequality story in \(k\). For the positive tangency value, find the point of tangency exactly and verify it lies on both graphs. Close with one sentence naming the three units this problem walked through.
📑 PART B — THE FUNCTION FAMILY FIELD GUIDE (one hand-drawn page)

On one single page, build a naturalist's field guide to every family of functions and relations you met this semester. Rule it as a chart — one row per family, four columns — drawn and lettered by hand. A field guide is meant for identification at a glance in the wild, so keep every entry compact and honest.

The rows (eight): linear · polynomial (degree \(\geq 2\)) · rational · exponential · logarithmic · radical (even index) · piecewise · the conic relations (circle, ellipse, parabola, hyperbola — one row, four thumbnail shapes, and a note on why most of them are not functions at all).

The columns (four):

  • Shape — a small, characteristic sketch: asymptotes dashed, launch points and intercepts dotted deliberately.
  • Domain — in interval notation, with a word on what restricts it (a denominator, an even index, a logarithm's argument, a border).
  • Long-run behavior — what the family commits to as \(x \to \pm\infty\): end behavior, asymptote, or (for the conics) the bounded or unbounded verdict.
  • Where the model can be trusted — one honest sentence per row: the kind of real situation this family models faithfully, and where its domain of honesty tends to end.

This page is Part B of your PDF — and the single best study artifact you will carry into the final. Make it the page you would want back.

✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
I attempted every problem in Part A, including the honors stretch, with visible work — and my Field Guide fills one page with all eight rows and all four columns.
☐
On the two method problems, the full discipline appears in order — guards with reasons, every step of algebra shown, every candidate judged against the original, both compositions run to completion, verdicts in sentences.
☐
Graphs and sketches are large and labeled; domains in interval notation; exact values kept before decimals throughout.
☐
Where I got stuck, I named the gap honestly on the page instead of leaving a blank or bluffing — and flagged it as a study target before the final.
📊 GRADING RUBRIC

Your teacher applies this rubric to the two ⭐ method problems (the rest of the set and the Field Guide are checked for honest completion). This same four-criterion standard governs every hand-graded item in this course, all year — including next week's final.

Criteria Excellent (4) Proficient (3) Developing (2) Beginning (1) Pts
Understanding of the material
4 pts		 Method fits the problem exactly; definitions, notation, and results are used correctly throughout. Method is sound with a minor conceptual slip that does not derail the solution. Partial grasp; the approach starts correctly but loses the thread. The method does not match the mathematics being asked. 4 pts
Quality of reasoning
4 pts		 Every step follows from the last; the why is stated, and verification is actually performed. Reasoning is mostly explicit; one link in the chain is asserted rather than shown. Steps are present but read as recipe-following; explanations restate what without why. Answer appears with little or no supporting reasoning. 4 pts
Presentation & precision
4 pts		 Clean vertical layout, aligned equals signs, labeled answers, exact values; graphs read as visual arguments. Organized and legible with small lapses in labeling or alignment. Readable but disorderly; the grader must hunt for the thread. Work is scattered or illegible; steps cannot be followed. 4 pts
Intellectual honesty
4 pts		 All work shown faithfully as done; uncertainty and gaps are named plainly; nothing is dressed up. Work is genuine; one rough patch is smoothed over rather than acknowledged. Some steps appear reverse-engineered from the answer rather than worked. The page misrepresents how the work was actually done. 4 pts
Total: 16 pts
