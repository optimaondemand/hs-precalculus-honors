Unit Test: Right Triangle Trigonometry
Test · 40 pts · Hand-written, single PDF upload
📋 BEFORE YOU BEGIN

This test gathers the whole unit into one sitting: degree–radian conversion, arc length and sector area, right-triangle solving, the special-angle unit circle, reference angles and the ASTC sign pattern, the 30-60-90 derivation, multi-step field problems, and one synthesis item reaching back to Unit 4. It has three parts: Part A (fluency), Part B (reasoning — write these as arguments), and Part C (one synthesis problem connecting this unit to Unit 4).

Ground rules. Set aside a quiet block of about 90 minutes and complete the test in one honest sitting. Work alone: no classmates, no AI tools, no solution websites — this is the one place where the mathematics must be entirely yours. A calculator may be used for arithmetic and inverse-trigonometric evaluation only (evaluating a trig ratio, or reading off an angle with the calculator's inverse sine/cosine/tangent key); every setup, derivation, reference-angle reasoning, and step of written argument must appear on your page — the calculator may check a number, never supply the reasoning. Lesson pages and notes stay closed.

Write the honor line at the top of your first page and sign it: “I completed this test on my own, without help from people, notes, or AI tools.” Intellectual honesty is a quarter of the rubric — and in the unit whose virtue is responsibility, honest work is the first obligation you owe your reader.

📝 PART A — FLUENCY (Lessons 6-1 through 6-10)

Neat vertical work for every problem — one step per line, equals signs aligned, exact values where the angle is special, labeled answers with units.

1
Degree–radian fluency: Convert \(315^\circ\) to radians, in terms of \(\pi\). Convert \(\dfrac{5\pi}{3}\) radians to degrees.
2
Coterminal, arc length, sector area: ① Find a positive coterminal angle in \([0^\circ, 360^\circ)\) for \(-750^\circ\), showing the addition of full rotations. ② Convert your answer to radians, in terms of \(\pi\). ③ A separate sector has central angle \(\dfrac{2\pi}{3}\) radians and radius \(9\) cm — find its arc length and its area, exact values in terms of \(\pi\).
3
Right triangle: In a right triangle, one leg has length \(14\) and the angle opposite that leg is \(38^\circ\). Find the hypotenuse, the other leg, and the third angle — then verify your two legs and hypotenuse satisfy the Pythagorean theorem.
4
Unit-circle special values: Evaluate \(\sin\dfrac{5\pi}{3}\), \(\cos\dfrac{5\pi}{3}\), and \(\tan\dfrac{5\pi}{3}\) exactly, stating the quadrant and reference angle you used.
5
All six functions: Evaluate all six trigonometric functions of \(\theta = 120^\circ\) exactly, stating the sign pattern before you write a single value.
6
Technology, checked: Evaluate \(\sec 250^\circ\) to four decimal places. First state the quadrant and reference angle and predict the sign, then compute and confirm your calculator's answer matches your prediction.
7
From one ratio, the rest: Suppose \(\sin\theta = 0.6\) and \(\theta\) terminates in Quadrant II. Using \(\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1\) — not a calculator's inverse function — find \(\cos\theta\) and \(\tan\theta\) exactly, choosing signs correctly for the quadrant.
8
A quick field fluency check: From a point \(40\) m from the base of a building, the angle of elevation to the top is \(55^\circ\). Find the height of the building.
📝 PART B — REASONING (write these as arguments)

These problems are graded primarily on the quality of your reasoning. Full sentences where explanation is asked; every claim earned, not asserted.

9
Derive the seeds themselves. Construct an equilateral triangle with side length \(2\), and drop an altitude from one vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side, splitting the triangle into two \(30\text{-}60\text{-}90\) triangles. ① Find the length of the altitude, using the Pythagorean theorem, showing every step. ② From the resulting right triangle, read off the exact values of \(\sin 30^\circ\), \(\cos 30^\circ\), \(\sin 60^\circ\), and \(\cos 60^\circ\) as ratios of sides, and state which side is opposite which angle. ③ Close with two sentences: these four numbers are the seed for one of the two non-\(45^\circ\) special-angle families you used all unit — name which reference angle they seed, and explain in your own words why deriving them once, geometrically, is more trustworthy than memorizing them as four unrelated facts.
10
Two-station field problem. From a point, the angle of elevation to the top of a tower is \(42^\circ\). A surveyor walks \(35\) m directly away from the tower and measures the angle of elevation a second time: \(24^\circ\). ① Declare your variables and set up one equation per station. ② Solve the system for the tower's height, every algebraic step shown. ③ Verify your height using the station equation you did not solve with first. ④ In two or three sentences, explain why a single station's measurement alone could never determine the height — what two unknowns does one equation leave undetermined, and what does the second station's equation supply that the first could not?
📝 PART C — SYNTHESIS (this unit meets Unit 4)
11
A wheelchair-access ramp must rise \(1\) ft for every \(12\) ft of horizontal run — the maximum slope the ADA allows. Let \(\theta\) be the ramp's angle of inclination, so \(\tan\theta = \dfrac{1}{12}\).
① Find the one angle \(\theta\) with \(0^\circ < \theta < 90^\circ\) satisfying \(\tan\theta = \dfrac{1}{12}\), to the nearest tenth of a degree. You may use your calculator's inverse tangent key as a tool, but state explicitly which key and what it returned.
② Widen the domain. Tangent is periodic with period \(180^\circ\). List a second angle, between \(0^\circ\) and \(360^\circ\), other than your answer to ①, that also satisfies \(\tan\theta = \dfrac{1}{12}\) — and name the quadrant and reference angle that produce it, citing ASTC to confirm tangent is positive there too.
③ Connect to Unit 4. In Unit 4 you learned that a function must be one-to-one — certified by the horizontal line test — before it can have a well-defined inverse. Explain, in three or four sentences, why "find the angle whose tangent is \(\frac{1}{12}\)" is not a well-defined question over tangent's full domain (cite your answer to ② as evidence that tangent fails the horizontal line test there), and explain what restricting to \(0^\circ < \theta < 90^\circ\) accomplishes — in Unit 4's own language, what property does tangent gain on that restricted interval that it lacks over all reals?
④ Close the loop. One sentence: why is \(0^\circ < \theta < 90^\circ\) not an arbitrary restriction here, but the one forced by the physical meaning of a right triangle's angle?
