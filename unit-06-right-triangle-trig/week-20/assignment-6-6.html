📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This is Week 20's Focus assignment — the one set this week your teacher grades for method. Everything in this lesson traced back to two cuts: a square along its diagonal, an equilateral triangle along its altitude. The two gold-badged ⭐ METHOD PROBLEMS ask you to make those cuts yourself, in full, and extract every exact value they produce — no shortcuts, no looked-up table. Your teacher reads them as arguments: the triangle built, the Pythagorean theorem applied correctly, every ratio read off the correct pair of sides, and a verification against the unit circle identity at the end. Give every problem honest work, and give the method problems the full derivation with nothing skipped because it felt obvious.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
A table of values can be memorized, mis-copied, or forgotten. A derivation, once understood, can always be rebuilt from scratch. What exactly does "derive it yourself" protect you from that "look it up" cannot?
📝 PROBLEM SET

Show your work for every problem in neat vertical form — one step per line, equals signs aligned, final answers labeled and rationalized. On every derivation, draw and label the triangle you are using. Exact values throughout; no decimals unless a problem explicitly asks for one.

1
Rescale the square: Bisect a square of side length 4 along its diagonal. Find the exact side lengths of the resulting right triangle, then state \(\sin\frac{\pi}{4}\), \(\cos\frac{\pi}{4}\), and \(\tan\frac{\pi}{4}\) in lowest terms. Compare your three values to Lesson 6-6's — what changed, and what didn't?
2
Rescale the equilateral triangle: Bisect an equilateral triangle of side length 6 along its altitude. Find the exact lengths of all three sides of the resulting right triangle, then state \(\sin\frac{\pi}{6}\), \(\cos\frac{\pi}{6}\), \(\sin\frac{\pi}{3}\), and \(\cos\frac{\pi}{3}\) in lowest terms.
⭐ METHOD PROBLEM — your teacher grades this one for method
3
Starting from an equilateral triangle with side length \(s\) (a general side, not a specific number): ① construct — draw the altitude from one vertex, and state the lengths of the two segments it creates, using the Pythagorean theorem to find the altitude in terms of \(s\); ② extract six values — state \(\sin\), \(\cos\), and \(\tan\) at both \(\frac{\pi}{6}\) and \(\frac{\pi}{3}\), fully simplified; ③ verify — confirm \(\sin^2\frac{\pi}{6} + \cos^2\frac{\pi}{6} = 1\) and \(\sin^2\frac{\pi}{3} + \cos^2\frac{\pi}{3} = 1\) with the computation shown; ④ explain why — in two or three sentences, explain why building the triangle with a general side \(s\) instead of a specific number (like 2) proves the ratios never depend on the triangle's size, and connect this to the idea of similar triangles.
4
A kite in the wind: A kite string makes an angle of \(\frac{\pi}{3}\) with the level ground, and the string itself is 100 m long. Find the kite's exact height above the ground and its exact horizontal distance from the flyer, then give both as decimals rounded to the nearest tenth of a meter.
5
Order without a calculator: Without computing a single decimal, order \(\sin\frac{\pi}{6}\), \(\sin\frac{\pi}{4}\), and \(\sin\frac{\pi}{3}\) from least to greatest. Justify your order using the triangles — which side gets longer as the angle grows — not a memorized ranking.
⭐ METHOD PROBLEM — your teacher grades this one for method
6
Starting from a square with side length \(s\) (general, as in problem 3): ① construct — draw the diagonal, and use the Pythagorean theorem to find its length in terms of \(s\); ② extract three values — state \(\sin\frac{\pi}{4}\), \(\cos\frac{\pi}{4}\), and \(\tan\frac{\pi}{4}\), fully simplified and rationalized; ③ combine families — using your \(\frac{\pi}{4}\) values and problem 3's \(\frac{\pi}{6}\) values, compute the exact value of \(\sin\frac{\pi}{4}\cos\frac{\pi}{6} + \cos\frac{\pi}{4}\sin\frac{\pi}{6}\), fully simplified as a single fraction; ④ bonus thread — this expression happens to equal \(\sin\frac{5\pi}{12}\) (\(75°\)), an angle no triangle in this lesson can reach directly. In one sentence, speculate on why combining two known angles' values in this particular pattern might produce the sine of their sum. (Unit 7 will tell you whether the guess is right.)
7
Explain why: In three or four sentences, explain why cutting a square along its diagonal always produces an isosceles right triangle whose hypotenuse is exactly \(\sqrt{2}\) times a leg, no matter how large or small the square is. Argue from similar triangles — the same idea problem 3 asked you to name.
8
Honors stretch — the hexagon confirms itself: A regular hexagon is inscribed in a circle of radius 10. ① Using the lesson's hexagon key fact, state the hexagon's exact side length, and explain why it equals the radius. ② One of the hexagon's six equilateral triangles has this side length; bisect it and find the exact legs of the resulting 30-60-90 triangle. ③ Using this triangle's own hypotenuse (not 2, and not the unit circle) as the base of the ratio, compute \(\cos\frac{\pi}{6}\) and \(\sin\frac{\pi}{6}\) from it directly, and confirm they match the unit-circle values from Lesson 6-6 exactly. ④ In a sentence, explain why this had to work out — what property of similar triangles guarantees it regardless of the circle's radius?
💡 CHECK YOURSELF WITH DESMOS
After both method problems are done on paper, verify your exact values in Desmos (desmos.com/calculator): type sin(pi/6), cos(pi/6), sin(pi/3), cos(pi/3), sin(pi/4), and cos(pi/4), and compare the decimal Desmos returns to your exact fraction or radical, evaluated by hand. They should agree to as many digits as you check. The calculator confirms the decimal; your algebra owns the exact value.

Quality of reasoning
4 pts		 Every step follows from the last; the why is stated, and the verification is actually performed. Reasoning is mostly explicit; one link in the chain is asserted rather than shown. Presentation & precision
4 pts		 Clean vertical layout, aligned equals signs, labeled answers, exact and rationalized values; triangles are drawn and labeled. Organized and legible with small lapses in labeling or alignment. Intellectual honesty
4 pts		 All work shown faithfully as done; uncertainty and gaps are named plainly; nothing is dressed up. Work is genuine; one rough patch is smoothed over rather than acknowledged.
