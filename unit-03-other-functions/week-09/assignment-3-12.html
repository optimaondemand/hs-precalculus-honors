📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This set puts Lesson 3-12's piecewise models to civic work: you will build a bracket tax code from its verbal description, prove its seams sewn shut, split marginal from effective rates, build a shipping model that jumps on purpose — and finish by designing a tax code of your own to an exact specification. All bracket schedules here belong to invented model republics: the mathematics is real; the figures are classroom fictions. This is a practice set: scored for honest completion, with worked solutions released after the due date for line-by-line self-correction.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
A policy is a piecewise function wearing a suit. Where are its breakpoints, do its seams jump or hold — and which of the two did the policymaker intend?
📝 PROBLEM SET

Show your work for every problem in neat vertical form — one step per line, equals signs aligned, answers labeled with dollar signs and percents. The republic of Bracketia taxes annual income as follows: 12% on income up to \$15,000; 22% on the portion above \$15,000 up to \$60,000; 32% on the portion above \$60,000. Problems 1–4 all use this code.

1
Build the code: Write Bracketia's tax function \(T(x)\) as a piecewise function, following the lesson's construction — each upper piece beginning with the settled tax of all brackets below it. Show the computation of each settled constant.
2
Three tax bills: Compute \(T(9{,}000)\), \(T(42{,}000)\), and \(T(100{,}000)\), naming which piece governs each and showing the arithmetic. For the largest income, also write its bill's anatomy: how many dollars of tax came from each bracket?
3
Prove the seams: Verify that \(T\) is continuous at both breakpoints by computing the two governing pieces at \(x = 15{,}000\) and at \(x = 60{,}000\) and showing they agree. Then state in one sentence what this continuity guarantees for a worker whose raise crosses a breakpoint.
4
Two honest rates: For the income \$100,000, state the marginal rate and compute the effective rate. Then write two sentences of interpretation: what different question does each rate answer, and why is the effective rate necessarily lower?
5
The jumping model: A parcel service in Bracketia posts this table — up to 1 lb: \$6; over 1 lb to 3 lb: \$11; over 3 lb to 8 lb: \$19; over 8 lb to 20 lb: \$34. Write the piecewise (step) cost function, graph it with correct endpoint dots, and state each weight where the price jumps and by how much. One sentence: why is a jump acceptable — even desirable — here, where it would be an injustice in the tax code?
6
Explain why: In two to four sentences, explain why the bracket language “on the portion above” forces the tax function to be continuous at every breakpoint — no matter what rates or breakpoints a republic chooses. (Hint: what is the tax on the portion above a breakpoint, evaluated exactly at the breakpoint?)
7
Honors stretch — design your own republic: Invent a three-bracket tax schedule (your own breakpoints and rates) that is continuous at both seams and charges exactly \$10,000 on an income of \$60,000. Present it as a piecewise function, prove both seam agreements, and show the computation \(T(60{,}000) = 10{,}000\). Many correct designs exist — the grade is in the verification, not the choice. One closing sentence: is your republic's code more or less progressive than Bracketia's at \$60,000, and how do you know?
💡 CHECK YOURSELF WITH EXCEL
After solving on paper, rebuild Bracketia in the lesson's workbook pattern: one nested-IF formula — =IF(A2<=15000, 0.12*A2, IF(A2<=60000, 1800+0.22*(A2-15000), 11700+0.32*(A2-60000))) — filled down a column of incomes. Test it at 14,999/15,001 and 59,999/60,001 to witness your problem-3 continuity numerically, and let an effective-rate column check problem 4. For the honors stretch, one edit of the rates and breakpoints audits your whole design in seconds. The spreadsheet confirms; your algebra convinces.
