📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This set puts Lesson 3-4's toolkit to work on the world: half-life clocks read in exact exponent form before any decimal, doubling models run at whole and fractional multiples, interest compared across frequencies, and one story told in both of its languages — sequence and function. Every model must pass its sanity check before it computes anything. This is a practice set: scored for honest completion, with worked solutions released after the due date for line-by-line self-correction.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
One chassis — an exponent that counts elapsed doublings or halvings — models bacteria, bones, medicine, and money. What must be true of a process for that single chassis to fit it?
📝 PROBLEM SET

Show your work in neat vertical form — one step per line, equals signs aligned, final answers labeled. For every decay and doubling answer, give the exact exponent form first (e.g. \(\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{20/8}\)), then the decimal. State units with every time variable.

1
Iodine-131, used in medical imaging, has a half-life of 8 days. Write the model for the fraction remaining after \(t\) days, run its sanity check at \(t = 8\), then find the fraction remaining after ① 20 days and ② 32 days — exact exponent form first, then a decimal (one of the two comes out as an exact fraction; say which and why).
2
Carbon-14's half-life is 5,730 years. An excavated wooden beam retains 78.5% of its expected carbon-14. Using the model \(\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{t/5730}\), show by direct evaluation that \(t = 2000\) years is consistent with the measurement — and explain in one sentence why your check is a verification, not a solution (what tool would solving for \(t\) require?).
3
A culture starts at 150 cells and doubles every 4 hours. Write the model with its sanity check, then find the population after ① 24 hours and ② 10 hours (exact form with a radical first, then the decimal, rounded to the nearest whole cell).
4
\(\$6000\) is invested at 5% for 6 years. Compute the balance with annual, quarterly, and continuous compounding — formula with numbers in place first, then the balance to the cent — and state the total spread between the bottom and top of this ladder in one sentence.
5
Two languages: \(\$800\) is deposited at 3.6% compounded monthly (0.3% per month). ① Sequence lens: write the recursion \(b_n = ?\) with \(b_0 = 800\), name the kind of sequence and its ratio, and give the explicit form. ② Function lens: write \(f(x) = ab^x\) with the same values. ③ Compute the balance after 18 months with either lens, and state in one sentence how the two lenses are related.
6
Explain why: The formula \(A_0\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{t/h}\) encodes the meaning of half-life. Explain in two or three sentences: what does the exponent \(\frac{t}{h}\) count, what happens at exactly \(t = h\), and what happens at \(t = 2h\)?
7
Honors stretch: Prove that a pure exponential \(f(t) = ab^t\) (with \(a > 0\), \(b > 1\)) has constant doubling time — that the time needed to double is the same from every starting moment. Do it without logarithms: compute the ratio \(\dfrac{f(t + T)}{f(t)}\), show it depends on \(T\) alone and not on \(t\), and conclude that whatever span \(T\) makes \(b^T = 2\) works from any starting point. Finish with the contrast: why does a linear function have no doubling time at all?
💡 CHECK YOURSELF WITH DESMOS
After solving on paper, audit problems 1 and 3 in Desmos (desmos.com/calculator): graph each model, add horizontal lines at your computed values, and confirm the intersections land at the times the problems named. Then test the lesson's staircase claim on problem 3 — lines at \(y = 300, 600, 1200\) should cross the curve at equal horizontal steps of 4. For problem 5, plot the sequence's monthly dots and the function's curve together and watch the curve thread every dot. The calculator confirms; your algebra convinces.
