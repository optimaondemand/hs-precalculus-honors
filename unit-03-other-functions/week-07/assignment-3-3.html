📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This is Week 7's Focus assignment — the one set this week your teacher grades for method. The two gold-badged ⭐ METHOD PROBLEMS are the lesson's two set pieces: the complete compounding ladder, presented as a table where every rung shows its formula, and a continuous-growth model built from a context and pressed until it asks a question you cannot yet answer exactly — which you must estimate graphically and name honestly. Give every problem honest work, and give the method problems the full presentation with nothing skipped because it felt obvious.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Compounding more often always earns more — yet a dollar compounded every instant for a year cannot reach $2.72. What shrinks faster than the refinements multiply, and what number stands at the ceiling?
📝 PROBLEM SET

Show your work for every problem in neat vertical form — one step per line, equals signs aligned, final answers labeled. Write every rate as a decimal before it enters a formula. Round money to the cent; keep six decimal places where a problem asks for them.

1
Evaluate \(\left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^n\) to six decimal places for \(n = 10\), \(n = 1000\), and \(n = 100{,}000\). State how far each still sits below \(e \approx 2.718282\), and describe in one sentence what the three gaps are doing.
2
Two quick continuous-growth computations with \(A = Pe^{rt}\), each with the model written first and the rate shown as a decimal:   ① \(\$4000\) at 3% compounded continuously for 12 years;   ② a herd of 120,000 growing continuously at 1.8% per year, after 25 years.
⭐ METHOD PROBLEM — your teacher grades this one for method
3
Build the complete compounding ladder for \(\$2000\) at 4% for 5 years. Present it as a neat table with one row per rung — annual, quarterly, monthly, daily, continuous — and three columns: the exact formula with numbers in place (e.g. \(2000\left(1 + \frac{0.04}{4}\right)^{20}\)), the balance to the cent, and the gain over the rung above. Then, below the table: ① in two or three sentences, explain why each refinement buys less than the last — what is already captured, and what shrinks; ② verify the ceiling — confirm that your continuous rung equals \(2000e^{0.2}\) and that no rung exceeds it.
4
Two banks court your \(\$5000\) for 10 years: Bank A offers 3.9% compounded monthly; Bank B offers 3.85% compounded continuously. Continuous compounding sounds unbeatable — is it here? Compute both balances to the cent, declare the winner, and state the moral in one sentence (rate versus frequency).
⭐ METHOD PROBLEM — your teacher grades this one for method
5
A laboratory culture starts at 500 bacteria and grows continuously at 8% per hour. ① Build the model \(A(t)\) from the context, stating what each constant means and showing the rate as a decimal; ② compute \(A(10)\) and \(A(24)\) to the nearest whole cell; ③ the lab must intervene when the culture reaches 2,000 cells — estimate when, by graphing \(y = A(x)\) and \(y = 2000\) in Desmos and clicking the intersection; report the estimate to the nearest tenth of an hour and sketch what you saw; ④ in one or two sentences, state explicitly what kind of answer this is (a measurement, not a solution), which equation an exact answer must solve, and what tool that solution awaits.
6
Explain why: In every ladder you have built, daily compounding and continuous compounding land within pennies of each other, while annual and daily can differ by many dollars. Explain in two or three sentences why the top of the ladder is so crowded — what has already been captured by the time compounding is daily?
7
Honors stretch: The lesson claimed that \(\left(1 + \frac{r}{n}\right)^n\) closes in on \(e^r\) — not just for \(r = 1\). Gather numerical evidence for \(r = 0.10\): compute \(\left(1 + \frac{0.10}{365}\right)^{365}\) and \(e^{0.10}\), both to six decimal places (Excel: =(1+0.1/365)^365 and =EXP(0.1)), and confirm they agree to four decimal places. Then say in one sentence what this means for the two interest formulas — why is \(Pe^{rt}\) the ceiling of \(P\left(1 + \frac{r}{n}\right)^{nt}\)?
💡 CHECK YOURSELF WITH EXCEL AND DESMOS
After the ladder in problem 3 is computed by hand, rebuild it in Excel: column A the compounding counts (1, 4, 12, 365), B2 =2000*(1+0.04/A2)^(5*A2) filled down, and =2000*EXP(0.2) beside it for the ceiling — every cell must match your hand table to the cent, and one mismatched cell means a rounding or exponent slip worth hunting. For problem 5, your Desmos intersection is the required estimate — but check it by substitution: plug your estimated \(t\) back into \(A(t)\) and confirm the output lands near 2,000. The calculator confirms; your algebra convinces.

📊 GRADING RUBRIC

Your teacher applies this rubric to the two ⭐ method problems (the rest of the set is checked for honest completion). This same four-criterion standard governs every hand-graded item in this course, all year.

Criteria Excellent (4) Proficient (3) Developing (2) Beginning (1) Pts
Understanding of the material
4 pts		 Method fits the problem exactly; definitions, notation, and results are used correctly throughout. Method is sound with a minor conceptual slip that does not derail the solution. Partial grasp; the approach starts correctly but loses the thread. The method does not match the mathematics being asked. 4 pts
Quality of reasoning
4 pts		 Every step follows from the last; the why is stated, and verification is actually performed. Reasoning is mostly explicit; one link in the chain is asserted rather than shown. Steps are present but read as recipe-following; explanations restate what without why. Answer appears with little or no supporting reasoning. 4 pts
Presentation & precision
4 pts		 Clean vertical layout, aligned equals signs, labeled answers, exact values; tables read as visual arguments. Organized and legible with small lapses in labeling or alignment. Readable but disorderly; the grader must hunt for the thread. Work is scattered or illegible; steps cannot be followed. 4 pts
Intellectual honesty
4 pts		 All work shown faithfully as done; uncertainty and gaps are named plainly; nothing is dressed up. Work is genuine; one rough patch is smoothed over rather than acknowledged. Some steps appear reverse-engineered from the answer rather than worked. The page misrepresents how the work was actually done. 4 pts
