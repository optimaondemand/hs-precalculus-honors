📋 BEFORE YOU BEGIN

This test gathers the whole unit into one sitting: complex-number arithmetic, plotting on the complex plane, distance and midpoint between complex numbers, conversion among rectangular, polar, and trigonometric forms, and De Moivre's Theorem for powers and roots. It has three parts: Part A (fluency), Part B (reasoning — write these as arguments), and Part C (one synthesis problem connecting this unit to Unit 8).

Ground rules. Set aside a quiet block of about 90 minutes and complete the test in one honest sitting. Work alone: no classmates, no AI tools, no solution websites — this is the one place where the mathematics must be entirely yours. A calculator may be used for arithmetic only (evaluating a decimal, checking a computation); every setup, every conversion, and every step of written argument must appear on your page — the calculator may check a number, never supply the reasoning. Lesson pages and notes stay closed.

Write the honor line at the top of your first page and sign it: “I completed this test on my own, without help from people, notes, or AI tools.” Intellectual honesty is a quarter of the rubric — and in the unit whose virtue is courage, honest work is the plainest form courage takes on a test.

📝 PART A — FLUENCY (Lessons 9-1 through 9-6)

Neat vertical work for every problem — one step per line, exact values kept exact, labeled answers.

1
Arithmetic: Let \(z_1=3-2i\) and \(z_2=-1+5i\). ① Find \(z_1+z_2\). ② Find \(z_1\cdot z_2\).
2
Plot and measure: Plot \(z=4-3i\) on the complex plane, and find its distance from the origin.
3
Distance and midpoint: Let \(z_1=1+2i\) and \(z_2=5-i\). Find the distance between them and their midpoint on the complex plane.
4
Rectangular to trigonometric: Convert \(z=3+3i\sqrt3\) to trigonometric form, showing the modulus, the reference angle, and your quadrant reasoning.
5
Trigonometric to rectangular: Convert \(w=5\left(\cos210^\circ+i\sin210^\circ\right)\) to rectangular form.
6
Product and quotient: Let \(z_1=3\,\mathrm{cis}\,40^\circ\) and \(z_2=2\,\mathrm{cis}\,110^\circ\). ① Find \(z_1z_2\) in trigonometric form. ② Find \(\dfrac{z_1}{z_2}\) in trigonometric form.
7
De Moivre power: Compute \((1+i)^8\) using De Moivre’s Theorem, showing your trigonometric conversion of \(1+i\) explicitly.
8
De Moivre roots: Find both square roots of \(z=2+2i\sqrt3\), and verify one of them by squaring it directly.
📝 PART B — REASONING (write these as arguments)

These problems are graded primarily on the quality of your reasoning. Full sentences where explanation is asked; every claim earned, not asserted.

9
Complex solutions to a real equation, interpreted. Consider \(x^2+4x+13=0\). ① Solve using the quadratic formula, showing the discriminant explicitly and simplifying \(\sqrt{-36}\) in terms of \(i\). ② Plot both solutions on the complex plane, and find the modulus of each. ③ In two or three sentences, explain why the two solutions are complex conjugates of one another, and what that tells you, geometrically, about where each root sits relative to the real axis. ④ State, in general terms, why a quadratic equation with real coefficients and a negative discriminant always produces a conjugate pair rather than two unrelated complex numbers.
10
All the roots, confirmed as a polygon. Find all four fourth roots of \(-16\). ① Show your full De Moivre root computation, cycling \(k=0,1,2,3\). ② Verify one non-trivial root by raising it back to the fourth power. ③ State the radius of the circle all four roots lie on, and the angle spacing between consecutive roots, and name the regular polygon their four vertices form. ④ In two or three sentences, explain why a real number's positive real fourth root (here, no real fourth root of \(-16\) exists at all) can still coexist with a full family of four complex fourth roots — what does "real" restrict that "complex" does not?
📝 PART C — SYNTHESIS (this unit meets Unit 8)
11
Consider the complex number \(z=-5-5i\).
① Convert \(z\) to trigonometric form using exactly the same conversion machinery Unit 8 built for rectangular-to-polar coordinate conversion: compute \(r\) from \(r^2=x^2+y^2\), find the reference angle from \(\tan\theta=\dfrac{y}{x}\), and then determine the correct quadrant for \(\theta\) by reasoning about the actual signs of \(x\) and \(y\) — not by trusting \(\tan\theta\) alone.
② Explain the trap. In one or two sentences, explain why \(\tan\theta=1\) for this number is not, by itself, enough information to determine \(\theta\) — name the other angle in \([0^\circ,360^\circ)\) that also has \(\tan\theta=1\), and explain how the signs of \(x=-5\) and \(y=-5\) rule that other angle out.
③ Verify. Substitute your trigonometric form back through \(x=r\cos\theta\) and \(y=r\sin\theta\) and confirm you recover \(z=-5-5i\) exactly.
④ Name the connection. One or two sentences: which specific lesson and technique from Unit 8 does this conversion reuse without any modification, and why does the fact that a complex number is secretly just a point in a plane make that reuse possible rather than coincidental?
📊 GRADING RUBRIC

The whole test is graded against the standing four-criterion rubric, each criterion scored out of 10. Part A supplies most of the evidence for understanding and precision; Parts B and C supply most of the evidence for reasoning.

Criteria What excellent looks like Pts
Understanding of the material Complex arithmetic, rectangular–trigonometric conversion, and De Moivre's Theorem for both powers and roots all executed correctly and fluently, with notation exact throughout. 10 pts
Quality of reasoning The conjugate-pair argument and the regular-polygon root argument both read as genuine arguments; the Part C connection to Unit 8's polar-conversion machinery is made explicitly and correctly, not merely gestured at. 10 pts
Presentation & precision Clean vertical work throughout, aligned equals signs, exact values kept in radical or \(\pi\)-form where called for, pages in order — forty points of work a stranger could follow. 10 pts
Intellectual honesty Honor line signed and honored; work shown as actually done; any problem you could not finish carries an honest note of where and why you stopped — which earns respect here, never punishment. 10 pts
