Unit Test: Trigonometric Functions
Test · 40 pts · Hand-written, single PDF upload
📋 BEFORE YOU BEGIN

This test gathers the whole unit into one sitting: graphing and transforming all six trigonometric functions, inverse trigonometric functions, periodic modeling, the Law of Sines and the Law of Cosines (including the ambiguous case), trigonometric equations, and the full identity toolbox — Pythagorean, sum/difference, double-angle, and half-angle/reduction. It has three parts: Part A (fluency), Part B (reasoning — write these as arguments), and Part C (one synthesis problem connecting this unit to Unit 4).

Ground rules. Set aside a quiet block of about 90 minutes and complete the test in one honest sitting. Work alone: no classmates, no AI tools, no solution websites — this is the one place where the mathematics must be entirely yours. A calculator may be used for arithmetic and inverse-trigonometric evaluation only (evaluating a trig ratio, or reading off an angle with the calculator's inverse sine/cosine/tangent key); every setup, derivation, identity substitution, and step of written argument must appear on your page — the calculator may check a number, never supply the reasoning. Lesson pages and notes stay closed.

Write the honor line at the top of your first page and sign it: “I completed this test on my own, without help from people, notes, or AI tools.” Intellectual honesty is a quarter of the rubric — and in the unit whose virtue is self-government, honest, disciplined work under no one's supervision but your own is the whole point.

📝 PART A — FLUENCY

Neat vertical work for every problem — one step per line, equals signs aligned, exact values where the angle is special, labeled answers with units.

1
Reading a transformation: For \(y = 3\cos\!\left(2\left(x-\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right)\right) - 1\), state the amplitude, period, midline, and phase shift. Then evaluate \(y\) at \(x=\dfrac{\pi}{4}\).
2
A reciprocal graph: For \(y=\sec(3x)\), state the period, and give the two vertical asymptotes nearest \(x=0\) (one positive, one negative).
3
Inverse trig, exact: Evaluate \(\arcsin\left(-\dfrac12\right)\) and \(\arccos\left(-\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\right)\) exactly, stating the range each inverse function is restricted to.
4
Basic trig equation: Solve \(2\sin\theta - 1 = 0\) for \(\theta \in [0, 2\pi)\).
5
Law of Sines: In triangle \(ABC\), \(A=40^\circ\), \(B=75^\circ\), and side \(a=12\) (opposite \(A\)). Find angle \(C\) and sides \(b\) and \(c\), rounded to the nearest hundredth.
6
Law of Cosines and area: In triangle \(ABC\), \(a=8\), \(b=10\), and the included angle \(C=50^\circ\). Find side \(c\) and the triangle's area, rounded to the nearest hundredth.
7
Pythagorean identity: Given \(\cos\theta = -\dfrac{5}{13}\) with \(\theta\) in Quadrant III, find the exact values of \(\sin\theta\) and \(\tan\theta\).
8
Sum formula, exact value: Find the exact value of \(\cos75^\circ\) using a sum or difference formula (write \(75^\circ\) as a sum of two special angles).
📝 PART B — REASONING (write these as arguments)

These problems are graded primarily on the quality of your reasoning. Full sentences where explanation is asked; every claim earned, not asserted.

9
Derive an identity from its roots. ① Starting from the sum formula \(\cos(A+B) = \cos A\cos B - \sin A\sin B\), set \(A=B=\theta\) and derive the double-angle identity \(\cos2\theta = \cos^2\theta-\sin^2\theta\). ② Using the Pythagorean identity \(\cos^2\theta = 1-\sin^2\theta\), rewrite your result as \(\cos2\theta = 1-2\sin^2\theta\). ③ Solve this equation for \(\sin^2\theta\), producing the reduction formula \(\sin^2\theta = \dfrac{1-\cos2\theta}{2}\). ④ Close with two sentences: which two identity families did this derivation connect, and why does deriving a formula this way — from formulas already proved — make it more trustworthy than memorizing it as an unrelated fact?
10
The ambiguous case, fully resolved. In triangle \(ABC\), \(a=15\), \(b=20\), and \(A=35^\circ\). ① Compute \(h=b\sin A\), and use it to determine how many triangles are possible — explain your reasoning using the comparison among \(h\), \(a\), and \(b\). ② Solve for every valid triangle: find all possible value(s) of angle \(B\), the corresponding angle \(C\), and side \(c\), rounded to the nearest tenth. ③ Close with two sentences: why does this problem have more than one answer, while a Law of Cosines (SAS or SSS) problem never does?
📝 PART C — SYNTHESIS (this unit meets Unit 4)
11
Consider the equation \(\sin\theta = 0.6\).
① Find the general solution. Using \(\beta=\arcsin(0.6)\approx36.9^\circ\) as a reference angle, write the complete family of real solutions to \(\sin\theta=0.6\), using \(k\) for an arbitrary integer.
② State what a calculator returns. Give the value your calculator's inverse sine key returns for \(\arcsin(0.6)\), to the nearest tenth of a degree, and identify it as exactly one member of the infinite family from ①.
③ Connect to Unit 4. In Unit 4 you learned that a function must be one-to-one — certified by the horizontal line test — before it can have a well-defined inverse. Using your two specific angles \(\theta\approx36.9^\circ\) and \(\theta\approx143.1^\circ\) (both members of the family from ①, both satisfying \(\sin\theta=0.6\)) as evidence, explain in three or four sentences why sine fails the horizontal line test over its full domain, and what restricting sine's domain to \([-90^\circ,90^\circ]\) accomplishes — in Unit 4's own language, what property does sine gain on that restricted interval that it lacks over all reals?
④ Close the loop. Cosine's inverse is instead restricted to the range \([0^\circ,180^\circ]\), not \([-90^\circ,90^\circ]\). In one or two sentences, connect this difference to the fact that sine is an odd function while cosine is an even function — why does an odd function's natural one-to-one interval sit symmetrically around \(0\), while an even function's does not?
