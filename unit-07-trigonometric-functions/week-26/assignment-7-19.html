Focus Set: When the Model Hides the Equation
Assignment · 16 pts · Focus (teacher-graded on the two method problems)
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This is Week 26's Focus assignment and the capstone of Unit 7: two full periodic-application problems where solving "when does this equal that" is impossible until an identity reduces the model first. The two gold-badged ⭐ METHOD PROBLEMS are complete field reports — a tide problem and a Ferris-wheel problem — and your teacher reads them the way this whole unit has trained: every identity named before it is used, every step shown, every solution set checked against the interval it was asked for. Give every problem honest work, and give the method problems the complete documentation discipline — nothing skipped because it felt obvious.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
A model that is disguised is not wrong — it is just not yet ready to be solved. What must happen to a disguised model before "solve for when it equals a given value" is even a meaningful instruction?
📝 PROBLEM SET

Show your work for every problem in neat vertical form — one step per line, equals signs aligned, labeled answers. On the method problems, name every identity before you use it, and check every solution against the stated interval.

1
Warmup — identity-assisted equation: Solve \(\cos 2\theta = \cos\theta\) for \(\theta\in[0,2\pi)\), naming the identity substitution you use.
2
Warmup — reduction formula: Find the exact value of \(\cos^2 105^\circ\) using a reduction formula (note \(105^\circ = \dfrac{210^\circ}{2}\)).
⭐ METHOD PROBLEM — your teacher grades this one for method
3
The tide report. A harbor's water depth, in meters, \(t\) hours after midnight, is modeled by \(d(t) = 5 + \cos^2\!\left(\dfrac{\pi t}{12}\right) - \sin^2\!\left(\dfrac{\pi t}{12}\right)\), for \(t\in[0,24)\). ① Reduce the model using a double-angle identity — name the identity, and show the substitution that turns \(d(t)\) into a single cosine function of \(t\). ② State the model's amplitude, midline, and period, and confirm the period is consistent with the harbor experiencing two high tides and two low tides each day. ③ Solve \(d(t) = 5.5\) for every \(t\) in \([0,24)\), showing the full general-solution reasoning before restricting to the interval. ④ Close with one sentence of physical reasonableness: do your four times fall into two sensible pairs (once on the way up to a high tide, once on the way down), and does that match what a real tide should do?
⭐ METHOD PROBLEM — your teacher grades this one for method
4
The Ferris wheel inspection. A carnival Ferris wheel completes one full revolution every \(18\) minutes; a car's angular position satisfies \(\theta = \dfrac{\pi t}{9}\), where \(t\) is minutes since the ride started (\(0\le t<18\), so \(0\le\theta<2\pi\)). Ride engineers must identify every instant in one revolution at which \(\cos 2\theta + \sin\theta = 0\), the condition their inspection checklist flags for a routine sensor log. ① Reduce the equation to one function using the appropriate double-angle identity — name it. ② Solve the resulting equation for \(\theta\) on \([0,2\pi)\), showing the factoring step in full. ③ Convert each \(\theta\)-solution back to a time \(t\) in minutes, showing the conversion \(t=\dfrac{9\theta}{\pi}\) for each. ④ Verify one of your three times by substituting it back into the original, undisguised equation \(\cos2\theta+\sin\theta=0\) — not the reduced form — and confirming it holds.
5
The AI cross-examination: Give your finished problem-4 work to your AI tutor with the instruction: “Check my double-angle substitution and tell me whether I found every solution on the interval.” Copy the AI's response onto your page (two or three sentences of it), then write your own verdict: is the AI's check correct? Verify every claim it makes before you rule — if it catches something real, say what you fixed; if its check is itself wrong (for instance, if it misses a solution, or suggests dividing by an expression that could be zero), refute it using the reasoning this unit has built. Either outcome earns full credit; what is graded is the judging.
6
Honors stretch — the same model, three disguises: Problem 3's tide model can be written three equivalent ways: \(d(t) = 5+\cos^2\!\left(\frac{\pi t}{12}\right)-\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\pi t}{12}\right)\) (the original), \(d(t) = 6 - 2\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\pi t}{12}\right)\) (a Pythagorean-identity disguise), and \(d(t) = 5+\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi t}{6}\right)\) (the fully reduced form). ① Starting from the original form, use the Pythagorean identity \(\cos^2\theta = 1-\sin^2\theta\) to derive the second disguise algebraically. ② Evaluate all three forms independently at \(t=3\) hours, and confirm all three give the identical number.
💡 CHECK YOURSELF WITH DESMOS
For problem 3, graph the original disguised form of \(d(t)\) and your reduced cosine form together in Desmos (desmos.com/calculator) — the curves should sit exactly on top of each other, confirming your reduction before you solve anything. Then graph y = 5.5 as a third line and read your four solution times directly off the intersection points. For problem 4, graph \(y=\cos(2x)+\sin(x)\) and confirm it crosses zero at exactly the three \(\theta\)-values you found.

📊 GRADING RUBRIC

Your teacher applies this rubric to the two ⭐ method problems (the rest of the set is checked for honest completion). This same four-criterion standard governs every hand-graded item in this course, all year.

Criteria Excellent (4) Proficient (3) Developing (2) Beginning (1) Pts
Understanding of the material
4 pts		 The correct identity reduces the model exactly; the resulting equation is solved completely and correctly. Method is sound with a minor slip that does not derail the solution. Partial grasp; the approach starts correctly but loses the thread. The method does not match the mathematics being asked. 4 pts
Quality of reasoning
4 pts		 Every step follows from the last; identities are named as they are used, and the verification/reasonableness check is actually performed. Reasoning is mostly explicit; one link in the chain is asserted rather than shown. Steps are present but read as recipe-following; explanations restate what without why. Answer appears with little or no supporting reasoning. 4 pts
Presentation & precision
4 pts		 Clean vertical layout, aligned equals signs, labeled answers throughout; the report reads as a document a stranger could act on. Organized and legible with small lapses in labeling or alignment. Readable but disorderly; the grader must hunt for the thread. Work is scattered or illegible; steps cannot be followed. 4 pts
Intellectual honesty
4 pts		 All work shown faithfully as done; uncertainty and gaps are named plainly; the reasonableness check tells the whole truth, including if something seemed off. Work is genuine; one rough patch is smoothed over rather than acknowledged. Some steps appear reverse-engineered from the answer rather than worked. The page misrepresents how the work was actually done. 4 pts
