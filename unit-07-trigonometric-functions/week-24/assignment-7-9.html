📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This set drills the Law of Sines on AAS and ASA triangles — the two data patterns that always produce exactly one triangle — plus one navigation-style application and one look ahead at tomorrow's ambiguous case. This is a practice set: scored for honest completion, with worked solutions released after the due date for line-by-line self-correction.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
The Law of Sines derivation never assumed a right angle anywhere. What, specifically, in the area-of-a-triangle argument makes that true?
📝 PROBLEM SET

Show your work in neat vertical form — every ratio labeled with the angle and side it pairs, final answers rounded to two decimal places unless the problem says otherwise.

1
ASA solve: Solve the triangle with \(B = 48^\circ\), \(C = 77^\circ\), and \(b = 26\).
2
AAS solve: Solve the triangle with \(A = 35^\circ\), \(C = 95^\circ\), and \(a = 18\).
3
Application: Two lighthouse keepers stand \(400\) m apart along a straight, level shoreline, points \(A\) and \(B\). Both sight the same offshore buoy at point \(C\). The angle at \(A\) between the shoreline and the sightline to the buoy is \(57^\circ\); the angle at \(B\) is \(64^\circ\). Find the distance from each lighthouse to the buoy.
4
Judge the claim: A classmate says, “The Law of Sines always gives exactly one triangle, no matter which two angles and one side you're given.” Judge the claim: is it true for AAS and ASA data specifically? What data pattern might make it false, and why (you do not need to solve anything — just identify the risk you'll meet tomorrow)?
5
Explain why: Starting from “Area \(=\tfrac12ab\sin C=\tfrac12bc\sin A=\tfrac12ca\sin B\),” explain in your own words, step by step, exactly how dividing through by \(\tfrac12abc\) produces \(\dfrac{\sin A}{a}=\dfrac{\sin B}{b}=\dfrac{\sin C}{c}\).
6
Honors stretch — the circumdiameter: For the triangle \(A=40^\circ, B=60^\circ, C=80^\circ, a=10\) (Lesson 7-9, Example 1), compute \(\dfrac{a}{\sin A}\), \(\dfrac{b}{\sin B}\), and \(\dfrac{c}{\sin C}\) using your own solved values of \(b\) and \(c\). Confirm all three are equal, and explain in one sentence what that common value represents about the triangle (a fact this circle-passing-through-all-three-vertices connection traces back to the same chord tables in today's historical opening).
💡 CHECK YOURSELF WITH DESMOS
After computing on paper, confirm problems 1–3 in Desmos (desmos.com/calculator) by typing each Law of Sines ratio directly, e.g. 26*sin(77°)/sin(48°). For problem 3, sketch the triangle first — labeling which angle is at \(A\), which at \(B\), and which side is \(c=400\) — before typing anything; a labeled sketch catches a mismatched ratio before the calculator ever gets the chance to.
